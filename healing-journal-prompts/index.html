70 healing journal prompts

Healing journal prompts are gentle questions that help you understand a hurt, let it out and slowly move forward. Below are 70 prompts for past pain, breakups, letting go, forgiveness, rebuilding trust in yourself and starting again. Go at your own pace and write in the private diary at the top of this page.

How to use journaling prompts for healing

Healing is rarely a straight line. Some days you feel lighter, and some days the old hurt shows up again. That does not mean you are going backward. Writing gives the pain a place to go so it does not have to sit only inside you.

  1. Start safe.Begin with the prompts about comfort and safety before the deeper ones. You decide how far to go.
  2. Write for a short time.Ten to fifteen minutes is plenty. Longer is not better when it comes to painful topics.
  3. Close the entry gently.End with something steadying: a comfort, a plan for the evening, or one kind sentence.
  4. Take breaks.If a prompt feels too heavy, skip it. You can come back in a week, a month or never.

Journaling can support healing, but it is not a replacement for professional care, especially after trauma or abuse. A therapist or counselor can help you work through what comes up safely. If you are in danger or in crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988.

Healing journal prompts to feel safe and grounded

  1. Where do I feel safest, and what makes it feel that way?
  2. Who are the people I trust with my real feelings?
  3. What small comfort can I keep close today, like a blanket, tea or a song?
  4. What does my body do when it starts to feel unsafe? What helps it settle?
  5. Describe a calm memory in detail: the colors, sounds and smells.
  6. What can I say to myself when old feelings come rushing back?
  7. Which places, people or topics do I need to step back from for now?
  8. What helps me come back to the present when my mind drifts to the past?
  9. What would a gentle, healing day look like for me?
  10. What is one thing that has stayed steady in my life through everything?

Journaling prompts for healing old hurts

  1. What hurt am I ready to look at today, even a little?
  2. What was missing back then that I needed most?
  3. How did that experience change the way I see myself?
  4. What story have I told myself about why it happened? Is that story fair to me?
  5. What did I do to survive that hard time? Can I thank that part of me?
  6. Which feelings about it have I never let myself say out loud?
  7. What do I wish someone had said to me back then? Write it now.
  8. How does this old hurt still show up in my daily life?
  9. What would healing from this look like, in small and real terms?
  10. What part of this is mine to carry, and what part belongs to someone else?

Healing journal prompts after a breakup

  1. What do I miss most, and what do I not miss at all?
  2. Write a letter to my ex that I will never send. Say everything.
  3. What did this relationship teach me about what I need?
  4. What parts of myself did I lose or quiet during the relationship?
  5. What am I looking forward to doing now that is just mine?
  6. Which friends can I lean on while I get through this?
  7. What signs did I notice that I want to trust sooner next time?
  8. How do I want to feel in my next relationship, whenever it comes?
  9. What would I say to a friend going through this same breakup?
  10. What is one thing I can do this week that has nothing to do with them?

Prompts for letting go

  1. What am I holding onto that is heavier than it needs to be?
  2. What would I gain if I let this go? What am I afraid I would lose?
  3. Write about this thing as if it happened a long time ago. How does that feel?
  4. What can I accept today, even if I do not like it?
  5. Which what-ifs keep pulling me back? Write each one, then cross it out.
  6. What is one physical thing I could give away, store or throw out to help me move on?
  7. What new thing could grow in the space this leaves?
  8. If letting go were a ritual, what would mine look like?
  9. What expectation of how things should have gone am I ready to release?
  10. What does my life look like six months from now if I keep loosening my grip?

Healing prompts about forgiveness

Forgiveness is a choice, not a rule. You do not have to forgive someone to heal, and forgiving does not mean what happened was okay.

  1. What does forgiveness mean to me? What does it not mean?
  2. Is there someone I want to forgive for my own peace? What is in the way?
  3. Can I let go of the hope for an apology that may never come?
  4. What would I say to the person who hurt me if I knew I was completely safe?
  5. What do I need to forgive myself for in this situation?
  6. How would my days feel if I stopped waiting for them to understand?
  7. Is there anyone I need to ask forgiveness from? What would I say?
  8. What boundaries do I need even if I forgive?
  9. What would make me feel ready to forgive, or is it okay not to?
  10. What can I release today, even if full forgiveness is not ready?

Journal prompts for healing and starting again

As you heal, you get to choose what comes next. Our self-love journal prompts and journal prompts for self-growth can help with this stage.

  1. What signs tell me I am healing, even slowly?
  2. What am I stronger at now because of what I went through?
  3. Who do I want to become on the other side of this?
  4. What new habit would support the person I am growing into?
  5. What brought me joy before the hurt that I could return to?
  6. What do I want to say yes to now that I could not before?
  7. How has my idea of what I deserve changed?
  8. Write a letter to myself one year from now, describing how I hope I feel.
  9. What would I like to tell someone who is at the start of a similar hurt?
  10. Which small thing made me smile this week without effort?
  11. What do I want my next chapter to be called?
  12. What new kind of people do I want around me?
  13. How can I honor what I went through without letting it define me?
  14. What does peace look like for me right now?
  15. What is one brave step I can take toward a life I want?
  16. What am I proud of myself for in all of this?
  17. Which part of my old self do I want to bring with me?
  18. What can I promise myself about how I will treat myself from now on?
  19. If my healing had a color, what would it be today?
  20. What do I want to remember the next time something hurts?

Tips for keeping a healing journal

  • Go slowly. One prompt a day, or even one a week, is enough.
  • Use your own words. You do not need to label what happened to write about it.
  • Notice your body. If your heart races or you feel numb, stop and do something grounding, like walking or holding something warm.
  • Look back with care. Reading old entries can show how far you have come. Only do it when you feel steady.
  • Keep it private. Your entries here stay in your browser on this device. Use the PIN lock on a shared computer.
  • Bring it to therapy. If you see a counselor, your journal can help you share what you have been carrying. See therapy journal prompts.

If your hurt is from losing someone, our grief journal prompts may fit better. For gentle work with the past, try inner child journal prompts or shadow work journal prompts.

Questions people ask

What is a healing journal?

A healing journal is a private place to write about something that hurt you, from a breakup to old family pain. You use it to name your feelings, understand what happened and slowly move toward peace. It can include letters you never send, lists of comforts, and notes about how you are changing.

How do I start a healing journal?

Begin with prompts that help you feel safe, like describing a calm place or listing people you trust. Then try one deeper prompt at a time. Keep entries short and close each one gently. You can use the private diary on this page or any notebook that feels safe to you.

Can journaling help heal trauma?

Writing can help some people make sense of painful experiences, and research on expressive writing has found it can help some people process stress. For trauma, though, it works best alongside a trained therapist who can help you feel safe while you work through hard memories. Journaling alone is not a replacement for that care.

What should I write when I am hurting?

Write what happened in plain words, how it feels in your body, and what you need right now. If that is too much, write one sentence and a comfort you can turn to. You can also write a letter you will never send. There is no wrong way to write about pain.

How long does healing take?

There is no set timeline. Some hurts ease in weeks, others take years, and feelings can come back on anniversaries or in new situations. That is normal. Writing regularly helps you see slow progress you might otherwise miss. Be patient with yourself and reach out for support when you need it.
