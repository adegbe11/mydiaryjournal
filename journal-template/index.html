What is in this free journal template

The page follows the shape of a day: how you start, what you plan, what happens, and what you take with you. Each box has one job.

  • Today I feel. One word and a reason. Naming a feeling is often the first step to handling it.
  • My top 3 for today. Three priorities, not a full to do list. If you only did these, the day would be a good one.
  • What happened today. The main events, like a short diary entry.
  • I am grateful for. Two or three things. Specific beats general: "my sister picked up the kids" instead of "family".
  • What I learned. One lesson, small or big.
  • Tomorrow I want to. One thing to carry into the next day, so each page connects to the next.

How to use the journal template

  1. Fill in the dateTap the line at the top of the page and type today's date.
  2. Do the top half in the morningWrite how you feel and your top three. It takes one minute.
  3. Do the rest at nightFill in what happened, what you are grateful for, what you learned and tomorrow.
  4. Print or save as PDFPress Print or save as PDF. Choose your printer, or choose Save as PDF to keep a digital copy.
  5. Clear and start againPress Clear for a fresh page tomorrow. Your answers save in this browser on this device until you clear them.

Journal template PDF, Word and Google Docs

There is no file to download. The page itself is the template.

  • PDF. Press Print or save as PDF, then pick Save as PDF as the printer. You can save it blank or filled in.
  • Word. Open a new document and type the six headings from this page, with a few empty lines under each. Save it as your own template.
  • Google Docs. Do the same in a new Google Doc. Make a copy of the doc each day, or add a new date heading at the top each morning.

A paper copy works well too. Print a stack of blank pages and keep them in a binder or clipboard by your bed.

Tips for using a journal template every day

  • Leave a box empty if you have nothing for it. The template is a guide, not a test.
  • Keep answers short. Two lines in each box is plenty.
  • Read back your pages at the end of the month. The "What I learned" lines are often the most useful.
  • If a box never feels right, change it. Swap "What I learned" for "A funny moment" if that suits you better.
  • Running out of things to write? Try our journal prompts or journal page ideas.

Questions people ask

Is this journal template free?

Yes. You can fill it in online, print it and save it as a PDF for free. There is no sign up and no email needed.

Where are my answers saved?

Your answers are saved in your browser on this device only. They are not sent anywhere. If you clear your browser data they will be gone, so print or save a PDF of pages you want to keep.

What should a daily journal template include?

A date, how you feel, your top priorities, what happened, what you are grateful for, what you learned and a note for tomorrow. That covers planning, remembering and reflecting in about five minutes.

How do I make my own journal template?

Choose four to six questions you want to answer each day, write them as headings with space under each, and keep the order the same every time. You can do this in a notebook, Word or Google Docs. Our journal page ideas list has many layouts to borrow.

Can I print the journal template?

Yes. Press the Print button and choose your printer, or choose Save as PDF. Print several blank copies at once and keep them in a binder.
