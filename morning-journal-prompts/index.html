Morning journal prompts to start your day

Morning journal prompts are short questions you answer when you wake up to check in with yourself and pick a focus for the day. Choose one prompt, write for five to ten minutes, and finish with one thing you will do today. Below are 68 prompts grouped by what you need most this morning.

How to use these morning journal prompts

A morning journal works best when it is short and the same every day. You are not writing a story. You are clearing your head and choosing what matters before the day starts choosing for you.

  1. Write before your phoneOpen your journal before you check messages or news. Even five quiet minutes change the tone of the day.
  2. Pick one promptScroll to the group that fits your mood. Tap Write next to any prompt and it loads into the page above.
  3. Write without fixingSpelling and neat sentences do not matter. Keep your hand moving until the timer ends.
  4. End with one actionClose with a single line: Today I will... This turns your thinking into something you actually do.

Morning check-in prompts

Start here if you feel foggy or off. These help you notice how you really are before you decide what to do.

  1. How did I sleep, and what is the first thing my body is telling me this morning?
  2. What was the first thought in my head when I woke up, and do I want to keep it?
  3. Rate my energy from 1 to 10. What would move it up by one point today?
  4. What am I carrying over from yesterday that I can set down before breakfast?
  5. Which three words describe my mood right now?
  6. What did I dream about, even if I only remember a small piece of it?
  7. What is one sound, smell or sight I notice in the room as I write this?
  8. If today were a weather forecast, what would it say?
  9. What am I quietly hoping happens today?
  10. What am I quietly dreading today, and how big is it really?
  11. What does my body need in the next hour: water, food, movement or quiet?
  12. What can I do in the first 10 minutes of the day that future me will thank me for?

Morning journal prompts for focus and intentions

Use these on busy days. They help you decide what matters most so the small stuff does not eat your whole day.

  1. What is the one task that would make today feel like a win if it were the only thing I did?
  2. How do I want to feel at 6 p.m. tonight, and what will help me get there?
  3. Which conversation today matters most, and how do I want to show up in it?
  4. What can I say no to today to protect my time?
  5. What is one small thing I have been putting off that I can finish before lunch?
  6. Pick one word to guide today. Why that word?
  7. What would I do today if I trusted myself a little more?
  8. Where will I take a real break today, away from screens?
  9. What is one way I can make today a little easier for someone at home or at work?
  10. If I only had four focused hours today, how would I spend them?
  11. What would good enough look like for my hardest task today?
  12. What will I eat today that makes me feel well, not just full?

Morning gratitude prompts

A few lines of thanks in the morning help you notice what is already good. For a full practice, see our gratitude journal prompts.

  1. What is one thing about this morning that I would miss if it were gone?
  2. Who made my life easier this week without being asked?
  3. What is one comfort I woke up with that many people do not have?
  4. Name something in my home I use every day and rarely notice. Why am I glad I have it?
  5. What part of my body works well today that I can thank?
  6. What is one thing from yesterday that went better than I expected?
  7. Which person am I looking forward to seeing or hearing from today?
  8. What is a simple pleasure waiting for me later today, like a meal, a show or a walk?
  9. What skill do I have now that I did not have a year ago?
  10. What is one thing I own that makes my mornings nicer?

Morning prompts for a better mindset

Some mornings start heavy. These prompts help you talk to yourself the way you would talk to a good friend.

  1. What story am I telling myself about today, and is it true?
  2. What would I tell a friend who woke up feeling exactly like I do?
  3. Write one kind sentence to myself that I can come back to at noon.
  4. What is something I am worried about that I cannot control? What part can I control?
  5. When did I last handle a hard morning well? What did I do?
  6. What do I need to forgive myself for so I can start today fresh?
  7. Finish this sentence three times: Today I am allowed to...
  8. What is one thing I can do today to make my space calmer?
  9. If I had a coach standing next to me this morning, what would they tell me to focus on?
  10. What would make me proud of myself tonight, even if the day goes sideways?
  11. What is one thing I am good at that I can lean on today?
  12. How can I respond, not react, if something annoying happens today?

Morning journal prompts for the bigger picture

Once or twice a week, zoom out. These connect today to the life you are building. For deeper work, try our journal prompts for self growth.

  1. What is one goal for this month, and what is today's small step toward it?
  2. Am I spending my mornings in a way that matches the life I want?
  3. What would my ideal morning look like, and which part of it can I do tomorrow?
  4. What is something I want to learn this year? Can I spend 15 minutes on it today?
  5. Who do I want to be more like, and what would they do today?
  6. What habit am I building right now, and how is it honestly going?
  7. What did I promise myself last week? Did I keep it?
  8. If this year had a title so far, what would it be?
  9. What is one thing I keep saying someday about? What would starting look like?
  10. What does a good life look like to me this season, in three sentences?

5 minute morning journal prompts

No time? Answer one of these in a line or two while the kettle boils. A short entry still counts.

  1. Today I will... (one line)
  2. One thing I am grateful for, one thing I am excited about, one thing I will let go of.
  3. My top three tasks, in order.
  4. My mood in one word, and why.
  5. Something I want to notice today.
  6. One person I will text or call.
  7. One way I will move my body.
  8. The one thing I will not do today.
  9. A sentence I want to hear today. Write it to myself.
  10. What would make today great?
  11. Water, sleep, food or people: which one needs care today?
  12. Tonight I want to be able to say...

Tips for a morning journal habit that sticks

  • Keep it tiny. Five minutes a day beats one hour once a month. You can always write more.
  • Tie it to something you already do. Journal while your coffee brews or right after you brush your teeth.
  • Keep the page ready. Leave your notebook open on the table, or keep our free online diary open in a browser tab.
  • Repeat prompts. Using the same three questions every morning is fine. Patterns show up faster that way.
  • Pair it with a night entry. Mornings are for intentions. Evenings are for looking back. Try our journal prompts before bed.
  • Read back on Sundays. A quick look at the week shows what you keep worrying about and what keeps going well.

Morning pages are a related idea from the book The Artist's Way: three pages of free writing first thing in the morning. If prompts feel too structured, try writing whatever comes out for three pages instead.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a morning journal?

Write how you feel, what you want from the day, and one thing you will do. A simple pattern is: a quick check-in on sleep and mood, one thing you are grateful for, and your top task for the day. If you get stuck, pick any prompt from the list above and answer it in a few lines.

How long should I journal in the morning?

Five to fifteen minutes is enough for most people. The goal is to clear your head and choose a focus, not to write a long essay. If you only have two minutes, answer one short prompt. A short entry you write every day is more useful than a long one you skip.

Is it better to journal in the morning or at night?

Both work, and they do different jobs. Morning journaling helps you set intentions and start calm. Night journaling helps you look back, let go of worries and sleep better. If you can only pick one, choose the time you can protect most days. Many people do a short entry at both times.

What are morning pages?

Morning pages are three pages of free writing done first thing in the morning, an idea made popular by the book The Artist's Way. You write whatever is in your head without stopping or editing. They are less structured than morning journal prompts, and some people use both.

Can I do a morning journal on my phone?

Yes. A phone or laptop works fine, especially if you always have it with you. Try to open your journal before you check messages, so other people's needs do not set the tone of your morning. Our free online diary saves pages in your browser with no sign up.
