How to use these Bible journal questions

Read a short passage twice. Then answer two or three prompts: one to understand it, one to see God in it, and one to apply it. That simple pattern works for any part of the Bible. If you want a full method, see how to journal the Bible for SOAP and other simple methods.

Bible journal prompts to understand any passage

  1. Summarize this passage in three plain sentences, as if explaining it to a friend.
  2. Who wrote this book, and who was it first written to?
  3. What happened just before this passage? How does that change what it means?
  4. Which words or phrases repeat? Why might the writer repeat them?
  5. Is there a word I do not understand? Look it up and write the meaning.
  6. What is the main point the writer wants the first readers to get?
  7. Are there any contrasts here, like light and dark, or old and new?
  8. What questions does this passage raise for me?
  9. Is there a cross reference in my Bible? What does that verse add?
  10. What would be lost if this passage were not in the Bible?
  11. Divide the passage into two or three parts. Give each part a short title.
  12. What feelings do the people in this passage seem to have?

Prompts to see God in the passage

  1. What does this passage show about who God is?
  2. What is God doing in this passage, even in the background?
  3. Is there a promise from God here? Who is it for?
  4. How does this passage point to Jesus or show His heart?
  5. What does this teach about how God treats people who fail?
  6. Where do I see God's patience, power or kindness here?
  7. If I only had this passage, what would I know about God?
  8. How does God respond to fear, doubt or sin in this passage?
  9. What does the Holy Spirit do or show here?
  10. Which word would I use to describe God after reading this?
  11. Write a short prayer of praise using what this passage says about God.

Prompts to apply the Bible to your life

  1. Is there a command here I need to obey this week? Write one practical step.
  2. Is there a sin to avoid or a habit to stop?
  3. Is there an example to follow, or one not to follow?
  4. Where does this passage touch my life right now: home, work, friendships, worry?
  5. What would change if I really believed this?
  6. Who in my life needs to hear the truth in this passage?
  7. How can I live this out before I read again tomorrow?
  8. What attitude does this passage ask me to change?
  9. What does this passage say to me in my current season?
  10. Which verse here should I memorize, and where will I put it so I see it?
  11. Turn this passage into a prayer for myself, using its words and ideas.

Bible journal prompts for the Psalms

  1. How is the psalm writer feeling at the start? How about at the end?
  2. What does the writer ask God for?
  3. Where does the writer turn from complaint to trust? What changed?
  4. Which line sounds most like my own heart today?
  5. What images of God does this psalm use, like shepherd, rock or shield?
  6. Rewrite this psalm in my own words as my own prayer.
  7. What does this psalm teach me about being honest with God?
  8. What reasons for praise does the writer give?
  9. If I wrote a psalm about this week, how would it start?
  10. What would it look like to wait on God the way this writer does?

Bible journal prompts for the Gospels

  1. Who comes to Jesus in this passage, and what do they want?
  2. How does Jesus treat the people others ignore?
  3. What question does Jesus ask, and how would I answer it?
  4. What does Jesus' miracle here show about His power and His care?
  5. How do the disciples misunderstand Jesus? Do I do the same?
  6. What does this parable mean in one sentence?
  7. Where do I see myself in this scene, as a disciple, the crowd or the person in need?
  8. What surprises me about how Jesus acts here?
  9. What does Jesus say about the kingdom of God in this passage?
  10. What would it have been like to stand in the crowd that day?
  11. What one thing from Jesus' words here will I practice this week?

Bible journal prompts for women

  1. Which woman in this passage, or in the Bible, do I relate to right now? Why?
  2. How does Jesus speak to women in the Gospels, and what does that tell me about how He sees me?
  3. What does Ruth's story teach me about loyalty and God's quiet care?
  4. What can I learn from Hannah about bringing my deepest desire to God?
  5. How did Mary respond to news that changed her whole life? How do I respond to change?
  6. What does Proverbs 31 show me about strength and wisdom, apart from busyness?
  7. Where am I like Martha, and where do I want to be like Mary?
  8. What does Esther's courage ask of me in my own place and time?
  9. Which verse speaks to my worth as a woman made in God's image?
  10. What would I tell a younger woman about this passage?
  11. Write a prayer for the women in my life using this passage.

Bible journal prompts for a deeper study

  1. Read the passage in two translations. What differences do I notice?
  2. Trace one key word through the whole book. Where else does it show up?
  3. Map the places mentioned. How far apart are they?
  4. What was daily life like for the people in this passage?
  5. How does this passage connect to the Old Testament or the New Testament?
  6. What would a skeptic ask about this passage, and how would I answer?
  7. Write a list of everything this passage says about faith.
  8. What does this passage teach about prayer?
  9. Find one verse in another book that says something similar. What does it add?
  10. What is the big story of the Bible, and where does this passage fit?
  11. Write a one paragraph summary of everything I learned this week.

Bible journal questions to end a week of study

  1. What do I want to study next because of this passage?
  2. Choose one word from this passage and journal on it every day this week.
  3. Write a short letter to God about what this study has meant to me.
  4. What would I teach a small group from this passage in five minutes?
  5. Which verse from this week will I write on a card and carry with me?
  6. What did I expect this passage to say, and what surprised me?
  7. Looking back at my notes, what is God teaching me again and again?
  8. What one question do I still have, and who could help me answer it?

Tips for using Bible journal prompts

  • Read the context. Always read the chapter around a verse before you write about it.
  • Mix your questions. One to understand, one about God, one to apply.
  • Keep a question list. Questions you cannot answer today are worth keeping.
  • Use a study Bible or dictionary for hard words and history.
  • Be consistent, not perfect. Five minutes most days beats an hour once a month.
  • Read your old entries. Notice what God has been teaching you over time.

Questions people ask

What are good Bible journal questions?

Good Bible journal questions help you understand the passage, see God in it and apply it. For example: What does this passage say in plain words? What does it show about God? Is there a command, promise or example? What will I do differently this week because of it?

How do you use Bible journal prompts?

Read a short passage twice. Choose two or three prompts, such as one about meaning, one about God and one about your life. Write your answers, then end with a short prayer. Repeat with the next passage tomorrow.

What are some Bible journal prompts for women?

Try: Which woman in the Bible do I relate to right now? How does Jesus treat women in the Gospels, and what does that show me? Where am I like Martha, and where do I want to be like Mary? See the full section for women on this page.

What book of the Bible is best to start journaling?

Short, clear books work best. Mark is a fast moving gospel. James is practical. Philippians is warm and encouraging. Psalms are great for prayer journaling. Read a few verses a day and journal on them.

How long should a Bible journal entry be?

There is no set length. A few lines that answer one good question is a real entry. On days with more time, you can do a longer study with several prompts. Consistency matters more than length.
