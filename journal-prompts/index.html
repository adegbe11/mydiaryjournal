Journal prompts

130 journal prompts to help you start writing

Journal prompts are short questions that give you something to write about when the page feels blank. Below are 130 journal prompts grouped by theme, from easy everyday questions to deeper ones about who you are. Pick one, press Write, and answer it in the diary at the top of this page.

How to use these journaling prompts

You do not need to answer every question, and you do not need to go in order. Read down the list until one makes you pause. That pause is a good sign. It means you have something to say.

  1. Pick one prompt.One is enough. If a question feels too big today, choose a lighter one from the everyday list.
  2. Press Write.The prompt loads into the diary at the top of the page. Your words stay saved in this browser on this device.
  3. Write for five minutes.Do not fix spelling or worry about sounding smart. Keep your pen, or your fingers, moving.
  4. End with one line.Finish with one thing you noticed or want to remember. That line is often the most useful part.

New to this? Read our guide on how to start a journal or try the gentle list of journaling prompts for beginners.

Everyday journal prompt ideas

These are quick and easy. They work well on busy days or when you want to keep a daily journal habit going.

  1. What was the best ten minutes of your day, and what made it good?
  2. Write down three things you heard today: a sound, a sentence and a song.
  3. What did you eat today that you would happily eat again tomorrow?
  4. Describe the view from the window closest to you right now.
  5. What took longer than you expected today, and why?
  6. Who did you talk to the most today? What did you talk about?
  7. What is one small thing you finished today, even if nobody noticed?
  8. Describe your mood right now as if it were the weather.
  9. What is sitting on your to-do list that you keep moving to tomorrow?
  10. What made you smile today without trying?
  11. If today had a title like a book chapter, what would it be?
  12. What did you learn today, big or tiny?
  13. Describe the outfit you wore today and how it made you feel.
  14. What is one thing you would do differently if you could replay today?
  15. What are you looking forward to this week?

Journal prompts about memories

  1. Describe the house you lived in when you were seven. Walk through it room by room.
  2. What was your favorite toy as a child, and where is it now?
  3. Write about a teacher who changed how you saw yourself.
  4. What smell takes you straight back to childhood?
  5. Describe the first time you felt truly grown up.
  6. What is the best meal you have ever eaten? Who were you with?
  7. Write about a trip that did not go as planned but turned out fine.
  8. What song was playing everywhere the summer you were sixteen?
  9. Describe a family tradition you loved, or one you were glad to leave behind.
  10. Write about a time you got lost, in a city or in life.
  11. What is a memory you replay when you need cheering up?
  12. Describe your first job and the people you worked with.
  13. What is something you believed as a kid that makes you laugh now?
  14. Write about a friendship from school. What happened to it?

Journal prompts to get to know yourself

These questions help you notice what you value and how you think. For a longer list, see our journal prompts for self-discovery and values journal prompts.

  1. What three words would your closest friend use to describe you? Do you agree?
  2. What do you do when you have a free afternoon and no plans?
  3. What topic could you talk about for an hour without notes?
  4. When do you feel most like yourself?
  5. What is a rule you live by that nobody taught you?
  6. What is something you changed your mind about in the last year?
  7. Which compliment do you remember years later? Why did it stick?
  8. What drains your energy faster than anything else?
  9. What do you secretly hope people notice about you?
  10. Describe your perfect ordinary Tuesday, from waking up to bedtime.
  11. What are you proud of that you rarely mention?
  12. What would your twelve-year-old self think of your life today?
  13. Which habit of yours would you most like to keep forever?
  14. What do you pretend to like that you actually do not?

Journal prompts for feelings and mental health

Writing about feelings can make them easier to name and carry. If you want more support, try our lists of journal prompts for mental health, journal prompts for anxiety and healing journal prompts.

  1. What feeling has shown up the most this week? When does it usually arrive?
  2. Where in your body do you feel stress first? What helps it loosen?
  3. Write about something that is bothering you, then write what you would tell a friend in the same spot.
  4. What do you need more of right now: rest, company, quiet or fun?
  5. What is a worry you can do something about this week? What is one you cannot?
  6. Describe a time you handled a hard moment better than you expected.
  7. What thought keeps looping in your head lately? Write it out in full.
  8. Which people leave you feeling calmer after you see them?
  9. What is something you have been carrying that you could put down?
  10. Finish this sentence five times: Right now I feel...
  11. What does a good day for your mind look like?
  12. When was the last time you cried? What was underneath it?
  13. What is one kind thing you can do for yourself tonight?
  14. List what is in your control today and what is not.

Tips for using a journal prompts list

  • Same time, same place. Writing after your morning coffee or right before bed turns prompts into a habit you do not have to think about.
  • Go sideways. If a prompt leads you somewhere else, follow it. The prompt is a door, not a rule.
  • Answer the same prompt twice. Pick a question you answered months ago and answer it again. Comparing the two is eye opening.
  • Keep it short on hard days. One honest sentence counts as journaling.
  • Make it printable. Use your browser's Print or Save as PDF to keep a paper copy of your answers, or grab our printable journal pages.
  • Keep it private. Your entries here are saved only in this browser on this device. No account, no sign up.

Stuck even with a prompt? Write: I do not know what to write because... and keep going. It works more often than you would think.

Questions people ask

What is a journal prompt?

A journal prompt is a question or short idea that gives you something to write about. It removes the hardest part of journaling, which is staring at a blank page. A good prompt is specific, like asking about the best ten minutes of your day, so you can start writing right away instead of wondering where to begin.

What should I write in my journal every day?

Start with three short parts: what happened today, how you felt about it, and one thing you want to remember or do tomorrow. On days you want more, pick a prompt from the list above. Even five minutes a day is enough to build the habit. Our guide on what to write in a journal has more ideas.

How many journal prompts should I answer at once?

One is usually best. A single prompt answered honestly gives you more than five answered in a rush. If you have extra time, answer one light prompt to warm up and one deeper prompt after that. Save the rest of the list for other days so it stays fresh.

Are journal prompts good for mental health?

Many people find that writing about their thoughts and feelings helps them feel calmer and see problems more clearly. Research on expressive writing has found it can help some people process stress. Journaling is a helpful habit, but it is not a replacement for care from a doctor or counselor when you need it.

Can I print these journal prompts or get them as a PDF?

Yes. Use your browser's Print option and choose Save as PDF to keep the full list. Anything you write in the diary on this page can be printed the same way. You can also use our journal template if you prefer to fill in a page on paper.

What if a prompt brings up hard feelings?

That is normal. You can stop at any time, switch to a lighter prompt, or close the page. Writing a few lines about what came up and one kind thing you can do next often helps. If you feel unsafe or in crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, you can call or text 988.
