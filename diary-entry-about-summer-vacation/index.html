Diary entry about summer vacation (6 examples)

A diary entry about summer vacation has the date and day at the top, opens with Dear Diary, describes one day or one trip from your holidays in the past tense, shares how you felt, and ends with a closing and your name. Here are 6 complete model entries, from a short one for young kids to longer ones for class 8 to 10, plus phrases, steps and a page to write your own.

Summer vacation diary entry format

The biggest tip for this topic: write about one day, not the whole holiday. A diary entry is written on a single evening about that day. If your question says "your summer vacation", pick the best day and tell it well.

  • Date, day and time at the top left.
  • Dear Diary, on the next line.
  • Where you were and who you were with in the first two lines.
  • What happened, in order, in the past tense.
  • How you felt and one thing you will remember.
  • Closing and name, such as Good night or Love, then your name.

If you need to cover the whole break, write it as the last evening of the holidays and look back: "Tomorrow school starts again. This summer I..."

Useful words and phrases for a summer vacation diary entry

Feeling words

  • excited, thrilled, carefree, relaxed, joyful, adventurous
  • homesick, bored, restless, sunburned and tired
  • grateful, proud, refreshed, a little sad that it is ending

Summer and holiday words

  • scorching sun, blazing heat, a cool breeze, long lazy afternoons
  • beach, waves, seashells, sandcastle, swimming pool, river, lake
  • hill station, campsite, village, cousins, grandparents, road trip
  • ice cream, mangoes, lemonade, watermelon, picnic, barbecue

Ready phrases

  • The holidays had finally begun, and I could not stop smiling.
  • We set off early in the morning with the car packed to the roof.
  • The view took my breath away.
  • I laughed until my stomach hurt.
  • Time flew by so fast that I could not believe the day was over.
  • It was a summer I will never forget.
  • I wish these holidays could last forever.

A few strong phrases plus your own real details work best. For more holiday ideas, see our list of diary entry topics.

How to write your own diary entry about summer vacation

  1. Choose one dayPick your best, funniest or most surprising day of the holidays.
  2. Write the date, day and time, then Dear DiaryThese go at the top, each on its own line.
  3. Say where you were and who was thereGrandparents' house, a camp, the beach, home. Name the people.
  4. Tell two or three moments in orderMorning, afternoon, evening works well. Use the past tense.
  5. Add senses and feelingsThe heat of the sand, the taste of the mango, how happy or tired you felt.
  6. End with a thought, a closing and your nameWhat will you remember? What are you looking forward to?

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing the whole holiday as a list. "We went here, then there, then there" is a list, not a diary. Focus on one day.
  • Writing it like an essay. An essay has a title and is written for readers. A diary entry is personal, dated and starts with Dear Diary.
  • No feelings. Say how the trip made you feel, not only where you went.
  • Wrong tense. Write what happened in the past tense. Use the future only for plans.
  • Forgetting the date, closing or name.
  • Going far past the word limit in an exam.

Questions people ask

How do I start a diary entry about my summer vacation?

Write the date, day and time, then Dear Diary. Your first sentence should say where you are and why the day was special, for example: Today was our first full day at the beach, and I woke up to the sound of the waves. Starting with a place and a feeling sets the scene quickly.

Should I write about the whole vacation or just one day?

One day is better. A diary entry is written on one evening about that day, so it feels real and focused. If the question asks about your whole summer, write it as the last evening of the holidays and look back on the best moments and how you have changed.

How many words should a summer vacation diary entry be?

Use the word limit in your question. Most class 6 to 10 exam questions ask for 100 to 150 words. Younger kids can write 60 to 100 words. For homework or creative writing without a limit, 200 to 300 words gives enough room for details and feelings.

What if I did not go anywhere during summer vacation?

That is completely fine. Write about a good day at home: learning a new skill, cooking with family, a game with friends, a book you loved, or a lazy afternoon. Examiners care about format, clear events and honest feelings, not how far you traveled.

What is the difference between a diary entry and an essay on summer vacation?

An essay has a title, is written for a reader and often covers the whole topic in general. A diary entry has a date and day, starts with Dear Diary, is written in the first person about one day, shares private feelings, and ends with a closing and your name.
