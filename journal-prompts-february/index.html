February journal prompts for every day

February journal prompts help you write about love in every form: romance, friendship, family and the way you treat yourself. Here is a prompt for each of February's 28 days, a bonus for leap day, and extra sets for Valentine's Day, self-love, friendship and the last weeks of winter.

How to use these February journal prompts

February is short, so it is a friendly month to try daily journaling. Answer the prompt for today's date in the diary above, or choose whichever one fits your mood. If Valentine's Day is hard for you this year, skip that set and use the self-love prompts instead. Nothing here assumes you have a partner.

Daily February journal prompts, from day 1 to leap day

Each number matches the date. February has 28 days, so prompt 29 is a bonus you can save for leap years or use on March 1.

Days 1 to 7

  1. What does love look like in your everyday life, in small actions rather than big words?
  2. It is Groundhog Day. If you could get six more weeks of something, what would you choose?
  3. Write about the first person outside your family who made you feel loved.
  4. What is a kind thing someone said to you that you still remember word for word?
  5. Describe your favorite way to spend a cold February afternoon.
  6. February is Black History Month in the US. Write about a person from Black history whose story moves you, and why.
  7. What is one way you show love that others might not notice?

Days 8 to 14

  1. Write about a friendship that changed who you are.
  2. What is something you love about yourself that has nothing to do with how you look?
  3. Which song makes you think of someone you love? Write about why.
  4. What is the best piece of relationship advice you have ever heard?
  5. Write about a time you felt truly understood by someone.
  6. Plan a small surprise for someone you care about this week. Who, what and when?
  7. Write about a love that is not romantic: a friend, a pet, a grandparent or a place.

Days 15 to 21

  1. What did Valentine's Day mean to you as a child, and what does it mean now?
  2. Presidents' Day falls in February. If you were in charge for one day, what is the first thing you would change?
  3. Do one random act of kindness today. Write about what you did and how it felt.
  4. What is one boundary that protects the love in your life?
  5. Describe a family tradition that shows love without anyone saying it.
  6. Who do you need to forgive, even a little, to feel lighter?
  7. What would your closest friend say is your best quality?

Days 22 to 28

  1. Write about a time you were brave for someone else.
  2. What is one way you could be gentler with yourself this week?
  3. Describe the first sign that winter is ending where you live.
  4. What do you wish people asked you about more often?
  5. Write about a time love meant letting go.
  6. What did you learn about kindness this month?
  7. Look back on February. Who made this month brighter, and did you tell them?

Day 29

  1. Leap day only comes every four years. What do you hope is different in your life by the next one?

Valentine's Day journal prompts

For couples, singles and everyone in between. For more, see our journal prompts on relationships.

  1. How did you meet your partner, or how do you hope to meet someone? Tell the story.
  2. What is your love language, and how do you know?
  3. Write about the most romantic thing anyone has ever done for you, big or small.
  4. If you are single this Valentine's Day, what do you enjoy most about this season of your life?
  5. What did your parents' or caregivers' relationship teach you about love?
  6. Describe your idea of a perfect date that costs less than ten dollars.
  7. What is something your partner or a close friend does that you never want to take for granted?
  8. Write a love note you could tuck into someone's coat pocket.
  9. What makes you feel most cared for when you are sick or tired?
  10. What kind of love do you want more of this year?

Self-love journal prompts for February

Love starts at home. See our full list of self-love journal prompts for more.

  1. Write yourself a Valentine. What would you say if you loved yourself the way you love your best friend?
  2. What part of your story are you finally ready to be proud of?
  3. List ten things you have survived.
  4. What do you need to hear today? Write it down as if someone else is telling you.
  5. Which of your flaws is actually a strength in the right setting?
  6. What does taking care of yourself look like on a busy weekday?
  7. Describe a moment this week when you were kind to yourself.
  8. What would you stop doing if you truly believed you were enough?
  9. Write about your younger self at age ten. What would you want them to know?
  10. What makes you laugh until your stomach hurts?

Friendship journal prompts

  1. Who is the friend you can call at 2 a.m.? Write about why.
  2. Describe the day you met your oldest friend.
  3. What is a friendship you lost, and what do you wish you had said?
  4. What makes someone easy to be friends with?
  5. Write about an inside joke and the story behind it.
  6. What kind of friend are you on your best day? On your worst?
  7. Who is someone you would like to get to know better this year?
  8. What did a friend teach you without meaning to?
  9. Plan a friend date for this month. Where would you go?
  10. Write a thank you letter to a friend who helped you through a hard time.

Late winter journal prompts

For the gray weeks when spring still feels far away.

  1. What is the hardest part of late winter for you, and what helps?
  2. Make a list of things to look forward to in March.
  3. Describe the light at five in the evening. Is it later than a month ago?
  4. What would make the rest of this winter feel a little lighter?
  5. Write about a winter you remember as especially hard, and how it ended.
  6. Which plant, flower or tree are you most excited to see again in spring?
  7. What could you do this weekend to break up the gray?
  8. Write a short note to spring, telling it what you will do when it arrives.

More February journal ideas

  • Keep a kindness log: one kind thing you did and one you received each day.
  • Write a short letter each week to someone you love, and decide later if you will send it.
  • Make a page of favorite lines about love and friendship. Our journal ideas with quotes can help.
  • Start a gratitude journal page just about the people in your life.
  • Save ticket stubs, notes and small keepsakes from the month for a junk journal.

Tips for journaling in February

  • Short month, short entries. Two or three sentences count.
  • Add a mood word. Write the date and one word for your mood at the top of each entry. It helps when you read back later.
  • Skip what hurts. If Valentine's Day brings up grief or loneliness, it is fine to skip it. Our grief journal prompts are gentle and private.
  • Ready for spring? Move on to March journal prompts when the month ends.

In the southern hemisphere, February is late summer. Swap the late winter set for a few of our August journal prompts.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in February?

February is a natural month to write about love and kindness. Write about the people you love, friendships that shaped you, and how you treat yourself. Valentine's Day, Groundhog Day and Black History Month give you easy starting points. In the last week, write about the end of winter and what you are looking forward to in spring.

What are good Valentine's Day journal prompts if I am single?

Write about what you enjoy about this season of your life, the friends and family who make you feel loved, and what kind of love you want in the future. You can also write yourself a Valentine. Being single on February 14 is a good time to notice all the love you already have.

How many daily prompts are there for February?

There are 29 daily prompts. February has 28 days in most years, so the last prompt is a bonus for leap day, which comes every four years. In other years you can use it on March 1 or on any day you want something different. There are also 38 extra prompts in the themed sets.

Can couples journal together?

Yes. Pick one Valentine's Day prompt, write your answers separately for ten minutes, then read them to each other. It often starts good conversations. Keep it kind and do not use it to argue. Each of you can also keep a private journal for the things you want to think through alone first.

What is a self-love journal?

A self-love journal is a place to write about yourself with the same kindness you would show a friend. You list your strengths, notice small wins, answer prompts about your needs, and practice talking to yourself more gently. It is not about being perfect. It is about treating yourself as someone worth caring for.
