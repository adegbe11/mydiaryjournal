105 prayer journal prompts to help you talk to God

Prayer journal prompts are short questions or sentence starters that help you begin a written prayer when you do not know what to say. How to use these prayer journal prompts

You do not need to answer every prompt. Pick one that matches how you feel today, read it slowly, and then write to God the way you would talk to a close friend. Two honest sentences are enough.

  • Start with a short Bible reading. A psalm or a few verses gets your mind on God before you write.
  • Write to Him, not about Him. Use "You" instead of "God" and the page turns into a real prayer.
  • Date every entry. Later you will want to see when you prayed and when the answer came.
  • Leave room for answers. Mark answered prayers in the margin, or keep them on the prayer journal template.

Prompts for praise and worship

  1. Write down three things about God's character that you saw in your life this week.
  2. Which name of God means the most to you right now, and why? Shepherd, Father, Healer, Provider?
  3. Describe something in nature you noticed today and praise God for making it.
  4. Write a short psalm of your own. Start every line with "You are..."
  5. When did you last feel God was close? Thank Him for that moment in detail.
  6. Praise God for one thing that has not changed in your life, even when everything else did.
  7. Read Psalm 103 and write your own list of His benefits to you.
  8. What would you tell a friend about who God is, in just five sentences? Say it to Him instead.
  9. Praise God for a promise He kept that you almost forgot about.
  10. Write about a song that helps you worship. What line in it is true for you today?
  11. Thank God for who He is, not for what He gives. What is left on the list?
  12. Picture heaven for a moment. What are you most looking forward to telling Jesus?

Prompts for gratitude and thanks

  1. Name one small thing from today you would have missed if you were not paying attention.
  2. Who has God put in your life this year that you did not expect? Thank Him for them by name.
  3. Write about a meal, a bed, or a roof you are grateful for tonight.
  4. Thank God for a hard thing that taught you something good.
  5. List five answered prayers you can remember from any year of your life.
  6. What do you have today that you once prayed for?
  7. Thank God for something your body did today, like walking, hugging, or healing.
  8. Write a thank you prayer for your church or a believer who helped you grow.
  9. Thank God for a door He closed. How did it protect you?
  10. What made you smile this week? Tell God about it as if He were there. He was.
  11. Thank Him for the gift of His Word. Which verse has carried you the most?
  12. End today with ten quick thank yous, one line each.

Prompts for confession and a clean heart

  1. Is there something you said this week that you wish you could take back? Bring it to God.
  2. Where have you been trusting yourself more than Him lately?
  3. Ask God to search your heart. Write down whatever comes to mind, without excuses.
  4. Who do you need to forgive? Write their name and ask for help to let it go.
  5. What habit is pulling you away from God? Name it plainly.
  6. Write about a time you felt guilty for a long time. Thank God that His mercy is new every morning.
  7. Where has pride shown up in your week? At work, at home, online?
  8. Is there someone you need to say sorry to? Ask God for the right words and the right time.
  9. What does it feel like to be fully forgiven? Write it out slowly.
  10. Write a prayer asking God to make your heart soft again in one area where it has gone hard.
  11. Which of your thoughts this week would you not want read out loud? Give them to God.

Prompts for worry, fear and hard days

  1. Write down everything you are worried about. Then next to each one, write "I give this to You."
  2. What is the worst thing you fear could happen? Tell God about it honestly.
  3. Where do you feel alone right now? Ask God to show you He is with you there.
  4. Write a prayer for strength to get through tomorrow, just tomorrow.
  5. What do you need from God today? Rest, money, peace, courage, a friend? Ask Him plainly.
  6. Tell God what you are angry about. He can handle your honest words.
  7. Write about a past storm God brought you through. What does that tell you about this one?
  8. What would you do today if you were not afraid? Ask God for the courage to take one step.
  9. Pray for your mind. Which thoughts keep looping, and what true thing can you replace them with?
  10. Write a prayer for your sleep tonight.
  11. Where are you waiting on God? Tell Him how the waiting feels.
  12. If you are grieving, write a letter to God about the person or thing you lost.
  13. Finish this sentence ten times: "Even if... You are still..."

If worry or sadness feels too heavy to carry, talk to someone you trust, a pastor, or a counselor. Prayer and professional help can go together. If you are in crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988. You may also like these Christian journal prompts for anxiety.

Prompts for praying for others

  1. Write a prayer for each person in your home by name, with one specific need each.
  2. Who is sick or hurting right now? Pray for their body, their mind and their faith.
  3. Pray for a friend who does not know Jesus. What do you hope God does in their life?
  4. Write a prayer for your pastor and church leaders this week.
  5. Pray for someone who hurt you. Ask God to bless them, even if it is hard to write.
  6. Who is lonely in your life? Pray for them, and ask God if you should call them.
  7. Pray for your neighbors, the ones you know and the ones you only wave to.
  8. Pray for your city, your leaders and your country. Name a real problem and ask for help.
  9. Pray for a missionary or a believer in a country where faith is hard.
  10. Write a prayer for the children in your family or church.
  11. Pray for someone who is grieving this season, like a widow or a family that lost a child.
  12. Who has prayed for you? Pray for them today.

For a page that is only about one person, try a prayer journal for your husband, a prayer journal for your children, or a prayer journal for your future husband.

Prompts for guidance and decisions

  1. What decision is in front of you? Write out both choices and ask God for wisdom on each.
  2. Where do you sense God may be leading you, even a little?
  3. Ask God what He wants you to do with this week. Write what comes to mind.
  4. What would it look like to trust God with your money this month?
  5. Is there a gift or talent God gave you that you are not using? Ask Him how to use it.
  6. Write a prayer for your work or studies. What do you need help with there?
  7. Ask God which relationship He wants you to put more time into.
  8. What are you holding onto that God may be asking you to open your hand about?
  9. Write about a time you felt God leading you clearly. What did it feel like?
  10. Pray Proverbs 3:5-6 over a specific plan you have right now.
  11. If you knew God was saying yes, what would you do next? If He was saying wait?

Prompts from Scripture

These prompts start with a passage. Read it first, then write. For more ideas, see Bible journal prompts.

  1. Read Psalm 23. Which line do you need most today, and why?
  2. Read Matthew 6:9-13. Rewrite the Lord's Prayer in your own words.
  3. Read Psalm 139:1-14. What does it mean to you that God knows you this well?
  4. Read Isaiah 40:28-31. Where are you tired, and what do you need to wait on God for?
  5. Read John 15:1-5. What does staying close to Jesus look like in your normal day?
  6. Read Romans 8:28. Write about something hard that you are asking God to use for good.
  7. Read Matthew 11:28-30. What burden can you hand to Jesus today?
  8. Read Psalm 46. What is shaking around you, and where is God in it?
  9. Read Lamentations 3:22-23. What mercy did you see this morning?
  10. Read Galatians 5:22-23. Which fruit of the Spirit do you want to grow in, and how?
  11. Read Philippians 4:4-8. Make a list of things that are true, lovely and good in your life.
  12. Read Luke 15:11-24. Where do you see yourself in the story?

Prayer journal prompts for women

These prompts were written with women in mind, from moms and wives to single women and busy workers. They work for anyone.

  1. Where do you feel stretched thin right now? Ask God for rest and help.
  2. Write a prayer about how you see yourself. Then write what God says about you.
  3. Pray for the women in your life: your mother, sisters, friends and daughters.
  4. What are you trying to control that you need to give back to God?
  5. Write a prayer for your home and the people who walk through its door.
  6. Where do you compare yourself to other women? Ask God to replace it with contentment.
  7. What dream have you set aside? Bring it to God honestly.
  8. Pray for your friendships. Which one needs care, and which one needs boundaries?
  9. Write a prayer for your body and your health this season.
  10. What does a quiet heart look like for you? Ask God to grow it in you.
  11. Write about a woman in the Bible you relate to and why.
  12. Thank God for one way He has made you strong.
  13. Pray for the woman you will be ten years from now.
  14. Ask God to show you one person you can encourage this week, and write her name.
  15. Write a prayer of rest. No requests, just being with Him.

Prompts for answered prayers and remembering

  1. Read an old journal page. Which prayers has God answered since then?
  2. Write about a prayer God answered with no. How do you see it now?
  3. Which prayer are you still waiting on? Tell God how long it has been and how you feel.
  4. Write the story of one answered prayer so your children or friends could read it someday.
  5. What has God done in your life this year that you want to remember forever?
  6. Make a list of times God provided exactly what you needed, right on time.
  7. Write a thank you prayer for an answer that came in a way you did not expect.

Tips for writing better prayers

  • Use one prompt a day. Too many at once turns prayer into homework.
  • Be specific. "Help Sam find a job near home by spring" is easier to watch God answer than "bless Sam."
  • Pray the same prompt again later. Your answer in six months may surprise you.
  • Keep a running list. Move names and needs onto a list you see often. Our prayer journal ideas show a few simple layouts.
  • Look back each month. Read old pages and write down how God answered, even when the answer was no or not yet.
  • Do not worry about pretty words. God reads the heart. Messy handwriting and short lines are fine.

Questions people ask

What should I write in my prayer journal?

Write to God the way you would talk to Him out loud. Many people start with praise or thanks, then confess anything on their heart, then pray for others and for their own needs. Add the Bible verse you read and leave a space to record answers. A prompt from this page can get you started on days you feel stuck.

How do you write a prayer in a journal?

Begin with a greeting like Dear Lord or Father. Tell Him what you are thankful for, what you are sorry for, and what you need, in plain words. Pray for other people by name. Close by trusting Him with it, and write the date at the top so you can look back later.

How many prayer journal prompts should I use a day?

One is plenty. The goal is honest time with God, not finishing a list. Some people pick one prompt in the morning and write for five minutes. Others use a prompt only when they feel stuck and write freely the rest of the time.

Can I use these prayer journal prompts with a group?

Yes. Pick one prompt for the week, let everyone write on their own, then share only what each person is comfortable sharing. Prompts about praise, thanks and praying for others work best in groups. Keep confession prompts for private time.

Are my written prayers on this page private?

Yes. What you write is saved in your browser on this device only. It is not sent to us and there is no account. If you want a paper copy, use Print or Save as PDF before you clear your browser data.
