Journal prompts before bed for a calmer night

Journal prompts before bed are short questions that help you look back on the day, get worries out of your head, and wind down for sleep. Pick one or two, write for about ten minutes, then close the page and put it away. Here are 64 night time prompts grouped by what you need tonight.

How to journal before bed

Night journaling is not about writing a perfect record of your day. It is about moving thoughts from your head onto the page so you do not have to hold them while you try to sleep.

  1. Dim the lightsWrite in soft light, ideally 20 to 30 minutes before you want to be asleep.
  2. Look back firstStart with one prompt about today so the day feels finished.
  3. Empty your headWrite down anything you are still chewing on, including tomorrow's to do list.
  4. End on something goodFinish with one thing you are grateful for or one kind line to yourself. Then close the journal.

If screens keep you awake, use a paper notebook at night, or turn your screen brightness all the way down and close the page as soon as you finish.

Prompts to look back on today

  1. What was the best ten minutes of today?
  2. What surprised me today?
  3. What did I handle better today than I would have a year ago?
  4. Where did my time actually go today? Does that feel right?
  5. What conversation from today is still playing in my head?
  6. What did I learn today, even something tiny?
  7. Who did I help today, and who helped me?
  8. What moment from today do I want to remember in ten years?
  9. What did I eat, drink and do today that left me feeling good?
  10. Which moment today felt the most like the real me?
  11. What drained my energy today, and was it worth it?
  12. If today was a chapter in a book, what would its title be?

Night time journal prompts to empty your head

Use these when your mind will not stop. Writing a worry down tells your brain it does not need to keep reminding you. If worry shows up most nights, our journal prompts for anxiety may help.

  1. List everything still bouncing around in my mind. Do not sort it, just get it out.
  2. What is one worry I can park on this page until morning?
  3. What will I handle tomorrow, and what can wait until later in the week?
  4. Is there something I meant to say today but did not? Write it here.
  5. What is the one thing I keep thinking I forgot? Write it down so I can stop checking.
  6. What is the worst that could happen with my biggest worry, and what is most likely?
  7. Which problem from today will matter in a month, and which will not?
  8. What do I need to ask for help with this week?
  9. If I could hand one task to someone else, which would it be?
  10. What would I tell myself at 3 a.m. if I woke up worrying?
  11. Write three things on tomorrow's to do list, then close the list.
  12. What feeling am I bringing to bed tonight, and where do I feel it in my body?

Gratitude prompts before bed

  1. Write three small good things from today, with one detail about each.
  2. What did someone say to me today that I liked hearing?
  3. What comfort am I enjoying right now: a warm bed, a quiet house, a full stomach?
  4. What part of today went fine that I usually take for granted?
  5. Which meal or drink today was the best, and why?
  6. What did nature show me today, even through a window?
  7. Who am I glad is in my life tonight?
  8. What did my body do for me today?
  9. What is one funny thing someone said today? Write it word for word.
  10. What is one thing about my home I am thankful for tonight?

Prompts to let go of the day

  1. What mistake from today can I forgive myself for right now?
  2. What did I take personally today that probably was not about me?
  3. Which feeling from today do I want to name and then release?
  4. Is there someone I am angry with? Write what I would say, then decide if it needs saying.
  5. What did I try to control today that was never mine to control?
  6. What expectation did I have for today that I can let soften?
  7. What would it feel like to truly believe I did my best with what I had today?
  8. What can I stop replaying tonight because I already learned from it?
  9. What do I want to leave on this page instead of taking into my dreams?
  10. Write a short goodbye to today.

Kind night prompts for yourself

  1. What did I do today that took courage, even a little?
  2. Where was I hard on myself today, and what would a kinder voice say?
  3. What do I like about who I was today?
  4. What need did I ignore today, and how can I meet it tomorrow?
  5. Write myself a goodnight note like I would write to a friend.
  6. What boundary did I keep today, or wish I had kept?
  7. How did I rest today, and was it enough?
  8. What is one way I showed up for someone I love?
  9. What am I proud of this week so far?
  10. What does enough mean for me tonight?

Night prompts to set up tomorrow

A little planning at night makes mornings easier. When you wake up, pick up where you left off with our morning journal prompts.

  1. What is the first thing I want to do when I wake up?
  2. What is one thing I am looking forward to tomorrow?
  3. What could go wrong tomorrow, and what is my simple plan if it does?
  4. What can I prepare tonight to make tomorrow morning calmer?
  5. Who do I want to reach out to tomorrow?
  6. What is the one task that will matter most tomorrow?
  7. What time do I want to be asleep, and what will help me get there?
  8. What mood do I want to wake up with?
  9. What will I do differently tomorrow than I did today?
  10. Finish this sentence: Tomorrow will be good if...

Tips for journaling at night

  • Keep it short. Ten minutes is plenty. Long, intense writing right before sleep can wake your brain up instead.
  • Save heavy topics for daytime. If a prompt brings up something big, write a line or two and come back to it tomorrow afternoon.
  • Use the same spot. Writing in the same chair or in bed each night becomes a signal that the day is over.
  • Write tomorrow's list, then stop planning. Once it is on paper, you are allowed to stop thinking about it.
  • Try a simple template. A daily diary page or a gratitude journal gives you the same few lines every night.
  • Keep it private. Our free online diary saves your pages in this browser, with no sign up, so you can write honestly.

Journaling can help you wind down, but it is not a treatment for sleep problems. If you have trouble sleeping most nights for weeks, talk to a doctor.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about before bed?

Write about what happened today, what is still on your mind, and one good thing from the day. A simple routine is a short look back, a brain dump of worries and tasks, and a line of gratitude. This helps the day feel finished so your mind can rest.

Does journaling before bed help you sleep?

It helps many people. Writing down worries and tomorrow's tasks can stop them from looping in your head when you lie down. Research on writing a to do list before bed has found it may help some people fall asleep a little faster. It is not a cure for insomnia, so see a doctor if poor sleep goes on for weeks.

How long should I journal at night?

About five to fifteen minutes. Keep it short enough that it relaxes you rather than stirs you up. If a topic feels too big for bedtime, write one line about it and promise yourself to come back to it the next day.

Should I journal on paper or on my phone at night?

Paper is easier on your eyes and keeps you away from notifications. If you prefer typing, lower your screen brightness, turn on night mode, and close the app as soon as you finish so you do not drift into scrolling.

What is a brain dump before bed?

A brain dump is writing down everything on your mind without sorting it: tasks, worries, ideas and feelings. Once it is all on the page, you can circle the few things that need action tomorrow and let the rest go for the night.
