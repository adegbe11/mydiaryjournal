July journal prompts: a prompt for every day

July journal prompts help you capture the heart of summer: fireworks, road trips, hot afternoons and long, slow evenings. Here is a prompt for each of July's 31 days, plus extra sets for the Fourth of July, vacations and summer adventures. Tap Write on any prompt to answer it in the diary above.

How to use these July journal prompts

Summer memories fade faster than you think, so July is a good month to write a little every day. If you are traveling, use the vacation set. If you are home, the adventure prompts will help you find something new close by. Your pages save in this browser, so you can keep writing from the same device all month.

31 July journal prompts: one for each day

Each number matches the date in July. Prompt 4 is for Independence Day.

Days 1 to 7

  1. July is the heart of summer. What do you want to make sure you do before it ends?
  2. Describe the hottest day you can remember. Where were you?
  3. What is your favorite summer smell: sunscreen, cut grass, a charcoal grill, or something else?
  4. It is Independence Day. What freedom in your life are you most thankful for?
  5. Write about the best fireworks you have ever seen.
  6. What does a lazy day look like for you, and do you allow yourself to have one?
  7. If you could spend a week anywhere this summer, where would it be and why?

Days 8 to 14

  1. Write about a time you got lost and found something good.
  2. What is the best thing you have cooked on a grill or eaten at a cookout?
  3. Describe a body of water you love: a river, lake, pool or ocean.
  4. What was your favorite summer toy or game as a child?
  5. Write about a time you did something brave in the summer, like jumping off a high dive.
  6. Who would you take on a summer road trip, and what would you play in the car?
  7. What summer song takes you right back to a certain year?

Days 15 to 21

  1. You are halfway through July. What has been the best moment of the month?
  2. Write about a sunburn, bug bite or summer mishap you can laugh about now.
  3. What do you love about where you live in summer?
  4. Describe a summer night when you looked up at the stars.
  5. What would you do with a whole day of no plans and no phone?
  6. Write about an adventure you had close to home.
  7. What do you notice about yourself on vacation that you do not notice at home?

Days 22 to 28

  1. Which summer movie, show or book would you recommend to a friend, and why?
  2. What is one thing you want to remember about this summer when it is cold in January?
  3. Write about a friend you only see in the summer, or a summer friendship from your past.
  4. Write about a farmers market, county fair or street festival you have been to.
  5. What is one thing you could do this week just for fun, with no goal at all?
  6. Describe the most beautiful view you have ever seen on a summer trip.
  7. Are you resting this summer, or just staying busy in a different way? Be honest.

Days 29 to 31

  1. Write about a summer that changed you.
  2. What do you want more of in August?
  3. Look back on July. List your top five memories of the month.

Fourth of July journal prompts

  1. How did your family celebrate the Fourth of July when you were growing up?
  2. What does being an American mean to you, or what does home mean to you if you came from somewhere else?
  3. Write about a cookout or parade you remember well.
  4. What is one freedom you think people take for granted?
  5. Who in your family history made sacrifices so you could have the life you have?
  6. What would you like your country to be like in 20 years?
  7. Describe the sound and feel of fireworks to someone who has never seen them.
  8. What does independence mean in your personal life right now?
  9. Write about a time you stood up for yourself.
  10. What is your favorite food at a Fourth of July party, and who makes it best?

Vacation and travel journal prompts

Bring these on the road, or keep a full travel journal for the trip.

  1. Write down three things you saw today that you have never seen before.
  2. What was the first thing you noticed when you arrived?
  3. Describe a local food you tried, and whether you would eat it again.
  4. Who did you meet on this trip, even for a minute?
  5. What went wrong on this trip, and how did you handle it?
  6. What does this place make you think about your life back home?
  7. Write about the moment on this trip you want to remember most.
  8. What did you pack that you did not need, and what did you forget?
  9. If you lived here, what would your normal day look like?
  10. Write a postcard to yourself from this trip.

Summer adventure journal ideas for July

No trip planned? These prompts work at home.

  1. Make a list of ten places within an hour of home that you have never visited.
  2. Write about the most spontaneous thing you have ever done.
  3. If you could learn any outdoor skill this summer, like kayaking or fishing, what would it be?
  4. Plan a backyard or balcony campout. What would you need?
  5. What childhood summer game could you play again with friends or kids?
  6. Write about a time you said yes to something you would normally skip.
  7. Describe your dream summer day from sunrise to midnight.
  8. What new food or recipe could you try this week?
  9. Write a story that begins: The ice cream truck stopped in front of my house and...
  10. What would a summer with no screens look like for you? Would you like it?

More July journal ideas

  • Sketch one thing you see each day. Our art journal ideas show simple ways to start.
  • Keep a summer log with the temperature and one highlight each day.
  • Write a top ten list of summer memories at the end of the month.
  • Ask family members for their favorite summer memory and write their answers down.
  • Use journal prompts for kids to keep children writing while school is out.

Tips for journaling in July

  • Write at the end of the day. Memories are freshest at night, even if all you write is a list.
  • Keep vacation entries short. Writing should never feel like homework on a trip.
  • Add the place. Write where you are at the top of each entry, next to the date.
  • Keep going in August. When July ends, continue with August journal prompts.

In the southern hemisphere, July is midwinter. Try the cozy winter prompts in our January journal prompts instead of the summer sets.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in July?

July is peak summer. Write about Independence Day, trips, hot days, cookouts and nights under the stars. It is also a good month to capture small memories before they fade, like the summer song you keep playing or the friend you only see this time of year. End the month with a list of your top memories.

What do you write about on the Fourth of July?

Write about how your family celebrates, a freedom you are thankful for, and what independence means in your own life right now. You can also describe the fireworks, the food and the people around you, so you can remember this year's Fourth when you read it back later.

How do I keep a journal on vacation?

Keep it short and easy. Each night, write the date, where you are, and three things you saw, ate or felt. Add one moment you want to remember. If you are using the online diary, write on your phone before bed. You can turn the notes into a longer trip story once you are home.

How can kids journal over the summer?

Give them one simple prompt a day, like the best part of today or a story that starts with the ice cream truck. Let them draw if they do not feel like writing. Keep it to ten minutes. Writing over the summer keeps their skills fresh and gives the family a fun record of the break.
