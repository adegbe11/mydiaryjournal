What is a prayer journal for women?

It is a place to pray in writing. Some women use a paper notebook, some use printed pages in a binder, and some use a prayer journal app on their phone. The format matters less than the habit: you sit with God for a few minutes, you read a little Scripture, and you write to Him the way you would talk to a close friend.

A prayer journal made for women often has room for the things that fill a woman's prayers: a husband or a future husband, children, aging parents, friends, work, church, health and her own heart. It usually gives a verse to start with and a few questions so you are never staring at a blank page.

Writing your prayers down does three simple things. It slows your mind so you can focus. It gives you a record of what you asked. And later, it shows you how God answered, which is the part most women say they treasure. If you are new to this, read what a prayer journal is and how to start one.

A short checklist

  • A verse to start with. A weekly or daily verse means you never have to hunt for where to read.
  • Room to write freely. Small boxes feel nice at first but get crowded on hard weeks.
  • A prayer list. A place to keep names and needs so you do not forget people.
  • An answered prayers page. This is what builds your faith over the years.
  • A pace you can keep. Weekly guides are easier to finish than daily ones for most busy women.
  • Privacy you trust. Prayers are personal. Paper can be locked away. An app should not show ads or ask you to make an account.

A simple weekly prayer journal flow

This flow takes about ten minutes a day. It follows one verse through the whole week, so the verse has time to sink in.

  1. Sunday: pick the verseChoose one verse for the week. Write the reference at the top of the page and read the whole chapter around it.
  2. Monday: write what it saysIn your own words, what does the verse say about God? Keep it to two or three lines.
  3. Tuesday: write your prayer listList the people and needs on your heart this week. Put one name or need per line.
  4. Wednesday: be honestWrite what you are struggling with or sorry for. Confess it plainly, then write that you are forgiven.
  5. Thursday: give thanksWrite three to five things you are thankful for, including one small thing from today.
  6. Friday: hand it overPick one worry from your list and write a prayer that gives it to God. End with one line about trusting Him.
  7. Saturday: look backRead the week's pages. Mark any answered prayers with the date. Write one sentence about what God taught you.

Miss a day? Just pick up where you are. A prayer journal is not a test. If you want more ways to set up your pages, see prayer journal ideas.

Prayer journal prompts for women

Use one prompt a day. Write the prompt at the top of the page above, or in your own notebook. There are many more in our full list of prayer journal prompts.

Your heart

  • Lord, this is what I have been carrying quietly that nobody knows about.
  • Where did I feel most like myself this week, and did I thank You for it?
  • What am I trying to control that I need to give back to You?
  • Which lie about myself keeps showing up, and what does Your Word say instead?
  • What would rest look like for me this week, in real, practical terms?
  • When did I last feel close to You? What was I doing?

Your home and family

  • Name each person in my home and write one thing they need from You this week.
  • Where do I need patience with someone I love, and what is making it hard?
  • Lord, help me speak kindly in this situation at home.
  • Which family member have I not prayed for in a while?
  • What is one way I can show love at home tomorrow that costs me something?
  • What do I hope my children, nieces, nephews or the young people around me learn about You from me?

Friends, work and church

  • Which friend is going through something hard right now? What would I ask You to do for her?
  • Where do I feel pressure at work, and what would trusting You look like there?
  • Who at church or in my town is lonely and needs to be seen?
  • Is there someone I need to forgive? Write her or his name and start the prayer.
  • How could I use my gifts to serve someone this month?
  • Who has been kind to me lately that I should thank, and thank You for?

Trust and thanks

  • List five ways You provided for me this year that I almost forgot.
  • What prayer have You answered in a way I did not expect?
  • What am I waiting for, and how can I wait with hope instead of worry?
  • Write a short thank you letter to God about this season of my life.
  • What promise in the Bible do I need to hold onto this week?
  • Finish this sentence ten times: Lord, You are...

Tips to keep your prayer journal going

  • Keep it where you will see it. On your nightstand, next to the coffee maker, or on your phone's home screen.
  • Set a small goal. Five minutes, four days a week, is a strong start.
  • Write like you talk. God does not need perfect words. Short, plain sentences are fine.
  • Use the same verse all week. You will notice something new each day.
  • Date every answered prayer. On hard days, read that list first.
  • Pray for others by name. It turns a worry list into an act of love.
  • Do not start over every January. Just keep going from today.

Questions people ask

What do you write in a prayer journal for women?

Start with a verse and the date. Then write how your heart is, what you are thankful for, who you are praying for and what you need help with. Close by handing one worry to God. When a prayer is answered, write what happened and the date. Over time this becomes a record of God's faithfulness in your life.

Is there a prayer journal app for women?

Yes. Our app, Prayer Journal for Women, gives you 52 weeks of Bible verses with reflection and a prayer page, plus a prayer list, answered prayers and a page for every day. It is coming soon on Google Play for Android. It has no ads, needs no account, and has no internet access, so your writing stays on your phone.

Is there a paper version of the app?

No. The app is for Android phones only. If you prefer paper, print the free page above as many times as you like, or use our printable prayer journal template. Many women do both: a phone for praying on the go and a printed page or notebook for quiet mornings.

How long should I spend on my prayer journal each day?

Five to fifteen minutes is plenty. A short time you keep is better than a long time you skip. Some women write a few lines each morning and a longer entry on the weekend. Pick what fits your season, and change it when your season changes.

Which Bible translation should I use for prayer journaling?

Use the one you understand and enjoy reading. The King James Version is loved for its beauty. The World English Bible is a modern, public domain translation that is easier for many readers. Our app lets you choose either one.
