How to write a diary about myself

To write a diary about yourself, write the date, then describe one thing that happened today and how it made you feel. Ask yourself why you felt that way, and end with what you learned or what you need. Be honest, because nobody else is reading. A few true lines about you are worth more than a page of what you think you should say.

How to write a diary about myself in 6 steps

  1. Pick a private place to writeYou will only be honest if you feel safe. Use a notebook you can keep somewhere private, or a private online diary like the one on this page.
  2. Write the date and how you feel in one wordTired. Hopeful. Annoyed. Calm. One word is a quick check in and helps you see patterns when you read back.
  3. Write about one moment from todayNot the whole day. Pick the moment that stuck with you. What happened, who was there, what was said.
  4. Ask yourself whyWhy did that moment matter? Why did it make you feel that way? This is where you start learning about yourself.
  5. Notice what you needRest, a talk with a friend, some time alone, a plan, a treat. Write it down.
  6. Close kindlyEnd with something you did well today, or something you are looking forward to. Write to yourself like you would to a good friend.

Starting is the hardest part. If you are stuck, write "Right now I feel..." and finish the sentence. Keep going from there.

How to write a diary about your day

Writing about your day is the easiest way to start writing about yourself, because the day gives you the story. The trick is to add you to it: not only what happened, but what you thought and did.

Just the factsAbout you
I went to work.I dragged myself to work. Mondays always feel heavy.
I had lunch with Tom.Lunch with Tom was the best part. I always feel lighter after we talk.
I watched TV.I watched TV for three hours to avoid thinking about the exam.

How to write a diary about your feelings

Feelings can be hard to put into words. These simple moves help:

  • Name it. Use a specific word. Not just bad, but lonely, embarrassed, disappointed or overwhelmed.
  • Find it in your body. Tight chest, heavy shoulders, a lump in your throat. Writing where you feel it helps you notice it sooner next time.
  • Find the trigger. What happened just before you felt this way?
  • Let it be messy. Write in fragments if you want. A diary is not an essay.
  • Ask what it is telling you. Anger can mean something felt unfair. Sadness can mean you miss something that mattered.

If you want to track feelings over time, a mood journal can help you spot patterns.

A diary can help you sort through hard feelings, but it is not a replacement for professional care. If you feel overwhelmed for a long time, talk to someone you trust or a doctor. If you are in crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988.

Prompts to write about yourself

Who you are right now

  1. Describe yourself in five words, then explain each one.
  2. What do people usually get wrong about you?
  3. What are you proud of that nobody else knows about?
  4. What does a really good day look like for you?
  5. What is something you used to believe about yourself that is no longer true?
  6. Which three people know you best, and what would each say about you?
  7. What are you curious about lately?
  8. What part of your personality comes from your family?
  9. Describe a place where you can fully relax and be yourself.
  10. What do you do when you are avoiding something?

Your feelings

  1. What emotion have you felt most this week, and where did it come from?
  2. What is weighing on you right now? Write all of it, then circle the part you can do something about.
  3. When did you last cry, and what was it really about?
  4. What makes you angry faster than anything else?
  5. What scares you about the next year?
  6. What helps you calm down when you are upset?
  7. What feeling do you hide from other people?
  8. Write about a time you felt completely safe.
  9. What would you say to a friend who felt the way you feel now?
  10. What is one thing you need to forgive yourself for?

Your past

  1. What is a memory that shaped who you are?
  2. What did you love doing as a child that you have stopped doing?
  3. Who was your hero when you were ten?
  4. What is the hardest thing you have been through, and what did it teach you?
  5. What is a decision you are glad you made?
  6. Write a letter to yourself at age eight.
  7. What advice would you give yourself one year ago?
  8. What moment are you most grateful for?
  9. Which place from your past would you visit again for one hour?
  10. What has changed most about you in the last five years?

Your future

  1. What do you want more of in your life? Less of?
  2. Describe your life five years from now on an ordinary Tuesday.
  3. What is one small habit that would make next month better?
  4. What are you putting off, and what is the first tiny step?
  5. What would you try if you knew nobody would judge you?
  6. What kind of friend, sibling or partner do you want to be?
  7. What would a good, successful year look like for you?
  8. What do you want to learn this year?
  9. Write a letter to yourself to open in one year.
  10. What do you hope never changes about you?

Tips for honest writing about yourself

  • Write as if no one will ever read it. Then make sure no one can. Keep the diary private.
  • Do not edit while you write. Get the thoughts out first. Spelling does not matter.
  • Be fair to yourself. Write about mistakes, but also write about what you did well.
  • Notice repeats. If the same worry shows up every week, that is worth paying attention to.
  • Read back once a month. You will see how you have grown, and what still needs care.
  • Keep it short on hard days. One line like "Today was hard and I got through it" is enough.

Questions people ask

How do I write a diary about myself?

Write the date and how you feel in one word. Then describe one moment from today, ask yourself why it affected you, and write what you need. End with something kind to yourself. Be honest and keep it private. A few true lines are a complete entry.

How do I start a diary about my feelings?

Start with the sentence Right now I feel, and finish it with a specific word like lonely, nervous or relieved. Then write what happened just before you felt that way and where you feel it in your body. Let it be messy. You can make sense of it later.

What should I write about myself in a diary?

Write about your day, your feelings, your worries, your hopes, your memories and the people who matter to you. You can also write about what you like and dislike, what you are proud of and what you want to change. Prompts help when you do not know where to begin.

Is it good to write a diary about your feelings?

Many people find it helps. Research on expressive writing has found that putting feelings into words can help some people process stress and see things more clearly. It is not a replacement for professional care if you are struggling, but it can be a helpful daily habit.

How do I write about my day in a diary?

Pick the moment that stuck with you most, write what happened, and then add how you felt and why. Adding your thoughts turns a list of events into a diary about you. If the day was busy, write three lines: what happened, how it felt, one thing to remember.
