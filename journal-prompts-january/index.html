January journal prompts: a prompt for every day

January journal prompts give you one simple question for each day of the month, so you can start the year with a steady writing habit. Below are 31 daily prompts for January, plus extra prompts for cozy winter days, goal check-ins and quiet reflection. Tap Write on any prompt to answer it in the diary above.

How to use these January journal prompts

Answer the prompt that matches today's date, or skip around. Five minutes is enough. If you missed a few days, do not try to catch up. Just start with today. January is long and quiet for a lot of people, which makes it a good month to build the habit. If you want a plan for the whole year first, start with our new year journal prompts.

31 January journal prompts: one for each day

The number of each prompt matches the date, so prompt 1 is for January 1 and prompt 31 is for January 31.

Days 1 to 7

  1. It is the first day of the month. What do you want to feel more of by January 31?
  2. Describe your space right now. What one change would make it feel like a fresh start?
  3. What did you do over the holidays that you want to remember?
  4. Write about the first time you saw snow, or the coldest place you have ever been.
  5. Which holiday decoration or tradition are you sad to pack away, and which are you glad to see go?
  6. What is one goal for this month that is small enough to start today?
  7. Write about something you are looking forward to between now and spring.

Days 8 to 14

  1. What does a cozy evening look like for you? Describe every detail.
  2. Who is one person you want to reconnect with this year? Write what you would say to them.
  3. What is your favorite thing about a cold morning, and your least favorite?
  4. Make a list of books, shows or films you want to finish this winter.
  5. What did you learn this week, about yourself or anything else?
  6. Write about a skill you have that you never give yourself credit for.
  7. What are you saying no to this January so you can say yes to something better?

Days 15 to 21

  1. You are halfway through the month. How is your January going so far, honestly?
  2. What is a winter food or drink that feels like home to you? Who first made it for you?
  3. Martin Luther King Jr. Day is a day of service in the US. What is one small way you could help someone this month?
  4. Write about a time someone stood up for what was right. What did you learn from watching them?
  5. What is a dream you have put on hold? What is one tiny step toward it?
  6. Describe the view from your window right now as if you were painting it.
  7. What helps you most on a gray, gloomy day?

Days 22 to 28

  1. Write about a time you started over. What did it teach you?
  2. What does rest really look like for you, not just sleep?
  3. Which of your habits from December would you like to keep, and which should go?
  4. Write a thank you note to your body for something it did this month.
  5. What is something you own that you have had for over ten years? Why have you kept it?
  6. What would you try if you knew no one would laugh at you?
  7. List ten small things that made you smile this month.

Days 29 to 31

  1. What surprised you most about this January?
  2. Write about someone who made this month easier for you.
  3. Look back at January. What worked, what did not, and what will you change in February?

Cozy winter journal prompts for January

For long, dark evenings when you would rather stay in.

  1. Describe the softest, warmest thing you own and where it came from.
  2. If you could hibernate for one week, what would you do when you woke up?
  3. Write about a snow day from your childhood, from the moment you heard school was closed.
  4. What candle, scent or sound makes your home feel cozy?
  5. Plan your perfect lazy Sunday in winter, hour by hour.
  6. What is one winter hobby you have always wanted to try, like knitting, puzzles or baking bread?
  7. Write about the sound of your home late at night in winter.
  8. Who would you most like to sit by a fire and talk with, living or not? What would you ask?
  9. What does winter teach you about slowing down?
  10. Describe a walk on a cold, clear day. What do you see, hear and feel on your face?

January goal check-in prompts

Use these in the third or fourth week, when the early energy of the new year starts to fade.

  1. Which of your new year goals still excites you, and which already feels heavy?
  2. What has been easier than you expected so far this year?
  3. Where did you slip this month? Write about it without judging yourself.
  4. If you could only keep one goal for the rest of the year, which would it be?
  5. What is one thing you could remove from your schedule to make space for your goal?
  6. Rewrite one goal so that it feels kinder and more realistic.
  7. What time of day are you most likely to follow through? Why?
  8. Who have you told about your goals? How did it feel to say them out loud?
  9. What would a good enough February look like?
  10. Write about one moment this month when you felt proud of your effort.

Quiet January reflection prompts

  1. What feels unfinished in your life right now, and does it need finishing?
  2. When do you feel most like yourself?
  3. What do you want people to feel after spending time with you?
  4. Write about a mistake that turned out to be a good thing.
  5. What are you curious about lately?
  6. Which season of life are you in right now: planting, growing, resting or harvesting? Explain.
  7. What is a kind thing you could tell yourself every morning this month?
  8. What is one thing you would like to say to someone, but have not found the words for yet?
  9. What does home mean to you right now?
  10. What is one question you hope this year will answer?

More January journal ideas

Not every page needs a prompt. Here are other ways to fill your journal this month.

  • Track the weather each day with one word and a small doodle.
  • Keep a winter reading log with a one line review of each book in a reading journal.
  • Start a gratitude journal with three lines each night before bed.
  • Write a short note each evening in a one line a day journal.
  • Make a list of ten things to do before winter ends, and check them off as you go.

Tips for sticking with January journaling

  • Tie it to something you already do. Write with your morning coffee or in the last ten minutes before bed.
  • Keep it short. Three honest sentences beat a page you dread.
  • Be gentle on gray days. If January feels heavy, choose the cozy and quiet prompts and skip the goal ones for a while.
  • Do not chase missed days. Pick up with today's date and keep going. Our daily journal prompts work any day of the year.
  • Move on in February. When the month ends, continue with February journal prompts.

Live in the southern hemisphere? January is summer for you. Keep the daily prompts and swap the cozy winter set for the summer prompts in our July journal prompts.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in January?

January is a good month to write about fresh starts, winter, and the goals you set for the year. Try looking back on the holidays, naming what you want to feel by the end of the month, and checking in on your goals in the third week. On cold, quiet days, cozy prompts about home, rest and comfort food are easy places to start.

How do I start journaling in January?

Pick one time of day, open a notebook or the free diary on this page, and answer the prompt for today's date. Keep it to five minutes at first. If you miss a day, skip it and answer today's prompt. Small, regular entries build the habit faster than long entries you only write once a week.

How long should a daily journal entry be?

As long as you want, and no longer. Three to five sentences is plenty for most daily prompts. Some days you will write a page, other days one line. What matters is showing up often. A short entry you actually write is worth more than a long one you keep putting off.

What if I miss a day of prompts?

Just skip it. Answer the prompt for today's date and keep going. If you liked a prompt you missed, save it for a weekend when you have more time. Journaling is not a test, and there is no score. Missing a few days is normal, especially in a busy month.

Are these January prompts good for teens?

Most of them work well for teens and adults. The cozy winter and goal check-in prompts are easy for teens to answer. For younger children, try our journal prompts for kids, which use simpler questions and shorter answers. Kids can also draw a picture first and write one sentence about it.
