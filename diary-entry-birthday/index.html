Diary entry on my birthday (6 examples)

A birthday diary entry gives the date and day, opens with Dear Diary, says how old you turned, tells the main moments of the day in the past tense (waking up, wishes, gifts, cake, friends), shares how you felt, and ends with a closing and your name. Below are 6 complete model entries, including one about a friend's birthday party, plus phrases, steps and a page to write your own.

Birthday diary entry format

A birthday entry follows the usual diary layout. The trick is to pick the moments that mattered most, so it does not turn into a long list of presents.

  • Date, day and time, for example: 3 March 2025, Monday, 10 p.m.
  • Dear Diary, on its own line.
  • Opening line: how old you are today and your first feeling of the day.
  • Main moments in order: morning, school or the party, the evening.
  • Feelings and thanks: who made the day special and why.
  • Closing and name, such as Good night, then your name.

Pick one gift or moment to describe in detail instead of listing ten. One clear picture is more memorable than a long list.

Useful words and phrases for a birthday diary entry

Feeling words

  • excited, overjoyed, thrilled, delighted, grateful, loved, special
  • surprised, speechless, emotional, shy, overwhelmed
  • a little sad, homesick, older, thoughtful

Birthday words

  • candles, cake, icing, frosting, sprinkles, balloons, streamers
  • presents, gifts, wrapping paper, greeting card, party bag
  • guests, surprise party, birthday song, wishes, celebration

Ready phrases

  • I woke up feeling that today was going to be special.
  • My family surprised me with breakfast in bed.
  • I closed my eyes, made a wish and blew out the candles.
  • I was speechless when I opened the present.
  • The house was filled with laughter and music.
  • It was the best birthday I have ever had.
  • I went to bed feeling loved and very full of cake.

Pick a few phrases and mix them with your own real details. For other topics to practice, see our list of diary entry topics.

How to write your own birthday diary entry

  1. Write the date, day and timeThen Dear Diary on the next line.
  2. Start with your age and a feeling"Today I turned eleven, and I woke up before the sun."
  3. Tell the day in orderMorning wishes, school or the party, the evening. Two or three moments are enough.
  4. Describe one special moment in detailA surprise, a gift, a funny accident, a kind word. Add what you saw, heard or tasted.
  5. Say how you feel tonight and who you are thankful forThis turns a list of events into a real diary entry.
  6. Close with a closing and your nameGood night, Love, or See you tomorrow, then sign it.

Common mistakes in birthday diary entries

  • Only listing presents. Describe one or two, then talk about people and feelings.
  • Forgetting your age. It is the first thing a reader wants to know.
  • Writing it as an invitation or a speech. A diary entry is private and looks back on the day.
  • Present tense for everything. Events go in the past tense: we cut the cake, I opened my gift.
  • Missing the date, closing or name. These are easy marks.
  • No ending thought. Finish with how you feel or a wish for the year ahead.

Questions people ask

How do I start a birthday diary entry?

Write the date, day and time at the top, then Dear Diary. Your first sentence should say your new age and how you felt, for example: Today I turned twelve, and I woke up so excited that I could not stay in bed. This tells the reader what the entry is about straight away.

What should I write in a diary entry about my birthday?

Write how old you turned, how the day started, the main moments such as school, a party or a family meal, one special gift or surprise described in detail, who made the day special, and how you feel at the end of the day. Finish with a closing and your name.

How many words should a birthday diary entry be?

Follow the word limit in your question. Class 6 to 10 exam questions usually ask for 100 to 150 words. Young children can write 60 to 100 words. For creative writing homework, 200 to 300 words lets you add more detail and feelings.

How do I write a diary entry about my friend's birthday party?

Use the same format: date, Dear Diary, body, closing and name. Say whose party it was and where, what gift you gave, two or three fun moments, and how you felt. Remember you are still writing about your own experience, so keep it in the first person.

Can a birthday diary entry be sad?

Yes. Birthdays are not always happy. You might miss someone, feel forgotten, or be away from home. Writing honestly about that is what a diary is for. Try to end with one small good thing or a hope for the year ahead, which also makes a stronger ending in an exam.
