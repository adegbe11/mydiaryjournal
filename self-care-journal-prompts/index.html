What self care journaling is for

Self care is not only bubble baths and face masks. Most of it is plain: going to bed on time, eating a real lunch, answering that one email so it stops nagging you. It is whatever helps you feel steady enough to handle your life.

A self care journal helps you notice what you need before you run on empty. Instead of guessing, you check in, write it down and make one small plan.

  • Check in first. Ask: how is my body, my mind and my mood today?
  • Pick one need. You cannot fix everything at once. Choose the one that would help most.
  • Make it tiny. A ten-minute walk you actually take beats a two-hour plan you skip.

Self care journal prompts for rest

  1. What does real rest look like for me, as opposed to just stopping?
  2. When was the last time I woke up feeling truly rested? What led to it?
  3. What is one thing I could cut from tomorrow to make room for a break?
  4. Which activities look restful but leave me more tired, like scrolling?
  5. What does a lazy Sunday morning look like in my perfect world?
  6. How many hours of sleep do I need to feel like myself? How many am I getting?
  7. What helps me switch off after work or school?
  8. Where in my home do I rest best?
  9. What would I do with a full day of nothing on the calendar?
  10. What signal tells me I need to stop before I am exhausted?

Prompts for physical self care

  1. How does my body feel right now, from head to toe?
  2. What meal makes me feel nourished and cared for?
  3. Which health task have I been putting off, like a checkup or dentist visit?
  4. What kind of movement did I enjoy as a kid? Could I try it again?
  5. How much water did I drink today? What would make it easier to drink more?
  6. What does my body crave this week: stretching, fresh air, warmth, quiet?
  7. Which small morning habit would help my body most?
  8. How does my posture feel at my desk or on my phone?
  9. What is one sense I can treat today: a good smell, a soft fabric, a favorite taste?
  10. What helps me feel clean, fresh and put together?

Self care journal prompts for your mind and feelings

For deeper questions, see journal prompts for mental health.

  1. What is taking up the most space in my head this week?
  2. Which feeling have I been pushing aside? What does it want me to know?
  3. What is one thing I can stop worrying about because it is out of my hands?
  4. Which news or apps leave me feeling heavy? Can I take a break from them?
  5. What hobby gives my mind a rest from everything else?
  6. What do I need to hear right now? Write it to myself.
  7. When did I last learn something just for fun?
  8. What would a quiet hour with no screens look like for me?
  9. Which thought pattern wears me out the most?
  10. What is a book, show or podcast that always comforts me?

Prompts for self care at home and in daily life

  1. Which corner of my home makes me feel stressed when I look at it?
  2. What is one fifteen-minute task that would make my space feel lighter?
  3. What could I prepare tonight to make tomorrow morning easier?
  4. Which bills, forms or emails are quietly stressing me out?
  5. What does a cozy evening at home look like for me?
  6. What smell, light or sound makes my home feel calm?
  7. Which routine would save me time and energy every week?
  8. What is one thing I can say no to this week to protect my time?
  9. What part of my day feels rushed? How could I slow it down?
  10. If my future self could thank me for one chore, which would it be?

Self care journal ideas for people and connection

  1. Who makes me feel lighter after I see them? When can I see them next?
  2. Which friend would love to hear from me this week?
  3. Is there a relationship I need a little space from right now?
  4. What kind of plan feels nourishing to me: a long talk, a walk, a meal, a game night?
  5. How can I ask for help with one thing this week?
  6. What is one way I could show care to someone without draining myself?
  7. Do I need more alone time or more company right now?
  8. Who in my life checks on me? How can I thank them?
  9. What group or community could I join to feel more connected?
  10. Which conversations leave me feeling heard?

Prompts for fun, play and joy

Fun is self care too. These help you put play back on your calendar. Our self-love journal prompts pair well with this list.

  1. What did I do for fun before life got busy?
  2. What would make this weekend feel like a mini vacation?
  3. Which song makes me want to dance? When did I last dance to it?
  4. What is a place nearby I have always wanted to visit but never have?
  5. What is a creative project I would start if nobody ever had to see it?
  6. What makes me laugh until I cannot breathe?
  7. Which treat feels special without costing much?
  8. What would I do on a day off if I did not feel guilty?
  9. What small adventure could I plan for this month?
  10. Write a list of twenty things that make me happy, as fast as I can.
  11. Which season activity do I look forward to most each year?
  12. What new thing would I like to try just to see if I like it?
  13. How can I add five minutes of play to an ordinary weekday?
  14. What does a perfect self care day look like for me from morning to night?
  15. What is one self care promise I will keep this week?
  16. Which of these self care ideas will I try first, and when?
  17. How will I know my self care is working?
  18. What would I tell a friend who never makes time for themselves?
  19. What gets in the way of my self care most often? How can I plan around it?
  20. What does taking care of myself look like on a really busy day?

A simple self care journal template

Copy these lines into the diary above each day, or print this page. They take about five minutes.

  1. Today my body feels...
  2. Today my mind feels...
  3. One thing I need more of today
  4. One thing I need less of today
  5. My small act of self care today
  6. Something I did well
  7. Tomorrow I will...

Tips for a self care journal

  • Keep a self care menu. Write a list of things that help, sorted by time: five minutes, thirty minutes, half a day. Pick from it when you are too tired to think.
  • Plan, then check back. Write your self care plan in the morning and how it went at night.
  • Notice what drains you. Self care is also about removing things, not only adding them.
  • Go easy on missed days. Skipping a day is not failing. Just pick it up again.
  • Write in the morning or at night. Try morning journal prompts or journal prompts before bed.

Questions people ask

What is a self care journal?

A self care journal is a place to check in with yourself and plan small ways to meet your needs. You write how your body and mind feel, what you need more or less of, and one simple thing you will do to look after yourself. It helps you spot burnout early and notice what really helps.

What should I write in a self care journal?

Write how you feel today, what you need, one act of self care you will do and how it went. You can also answer prompts about rest, health, home, friends and fun. Keeping a running list of things that help you feel better is useful on days when you cannot think of anything.

What are some self care journal ideas?

Ideas include a daily check-in page, a self care menu of quick and longer activities, a list of things that drain you, a sleep log, a weekly fun plan and a list of people who lift you up. The template and prompts above give you a simple place to start.

How often should I write in a self care journal?

A short check-in every day works well because your needs change from day to day. If daily feels like too much, try three times a week or once on Sunday to plan the week ahead. The best rhythm is the one you will actually keep.

Is self care the same as self love?

They are close cousins. Self care is mostly about actions, like resting, eating well and setting limits. Self love is more about how you see and speak to yourself. Each helps the other. Our self-love journal prompts focus on the inside part.
