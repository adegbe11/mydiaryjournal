Journal prompts

Daily journal prompts for every day of the week

Daily journal prompts are short questions you can answer in a few minutes each day to build a writing habit. The easiest way to use them is to give each day of the week a theme, so you never wonder what to write. Below are 84 prompts: ten or more for each day from Monday to Sunday, plus quick one line prompts of the day.

Monday: prompts for a fresh start

  1. What would make this week feel successful by Friday night?
  2. Which appointment or deadline this week needs the most attention?
  3. What did I leave unfinished last week that I want to close out?
  4. How full is my week on a scale of 1 to 10, and is that okay?
  5. What is one thing I can remove from my calendar this week?
  6. What is one fun thing I can put in this week so it is not all work?
  7. How do I want to treat my body this week?
  8. Who do I need to check in with this week?
  9. What is my Monday mood, honestly?
  10. What would I tell myself on Sunday night if I could reach back?
  11. Which meals can I plan now to make the week easier?

Tuesday: journal prompts about people

  1. Who did I talk to today that I do not usually talk to?
  2. Who do I owe a thank you note or message?
  3. Which friend have I not heard from in a while, and why?
  4. What is one thing I admire about a coworker or classmate?
  5. Who made me laugh this week?
  6. What is the nicest thing a stranger has ever done for me?
  7. Who do I feel most relaxed around, and what do they do that helps?
  8. Who would I like to invite over or meet up with soon?
  9. What did I learn from someone today?
  10. Who would I call first with good news?
  11. Describe one conversation from today in a few lines. What was under the words?

Wednesday: body and health prompts

A midweek check on how you are really doing. If you want to track this every day, try a mood journal or a fitness journal.

  1. How much water have I had today, and how do I feel?
  2. What did my body enjoy today: a stretch, a walk, a nap, a good meal?
  3. Where am I holding tension right now?
  4. How did I sleep this week overall?
  5. What food makes me feel strong, and when did I last eat it?
  6. What kind of movement do I actually enjoy, not just put up with?
  7. When did I last spend more than 20 minutes outside?
  8. What would my doctor tell me to do more of? Less of?
  9. How is my screen time affecting how I feel?
  10. What is one health habit I want to try for the rest of this week?

Thursday: prompts for learning and curiosity

  1. What did I wonder about today?
  2. What is a topic I could talk about for an hour?
  3. What is a question I want answered this week?
  4. What did I look up online today, and why?
  5. What book, podcast or show taught me something lately?
  6. What did I get wrong today, and what did it teach me?
  7. If I could take a free class in anything, what would it be?
  8. What is a skill I see someone else do that I would like to try?
  9. What is one new word, fact or idea I picked up this week?
  10. What surprised me about how something works?

Friday: daily prompts to count your wins

  1. What are three wins from this week, big or small?
  2. What did I finish this week?
  3. What got easier this week than it was last week?
  4. Who helped me this week, and did I thank them?
  5. Where did I show patience this week?
  6. What problem did I solve this week?
  7. What did I say no to this week that I am glad about?
  8. What do I want to celebrate tonight, and how?
  9. What compliment did I get or give this week?
  10. What lesson from this week do I want to remember?

Saturday: fun journal prompts

  1. What would I do today if I had zero chores?
  2. What is a free activity in my area I keep meaning to try?
  3. What is a game, puzzle or craft I could do this afternoon just for fun?
  4. What makes me lose track of time in a good way?
  5. What would a perfect lazy afternoon look like?
  6. What song is stuck in my head, and what does it remind me of?
  7. What is something silly I did recently?
  8. Plan a tiny adventure that costs under 20 dollars.
  9. What is a hobby I gave up, and do I miss it?
  10. Who would I like to spend a free day with?

Sunday: prompts to rest and reflect

Sunday is a good day to look back. For deeper questions, see our reflective journal prompts.

  1. How did this week compare to how I planned it?
  2. What did I need more of this week?
  3. What am I bringing into next week, and what am I leaving here?
  4. What part of this week felt most peaceful?
  5. What did I learn about my limits this week?
  6. Is my home ready for Monday? What one small task would help?
  7. Which day this week was the hardest, and what got me through it?
  8. What do I want next week to feel like?
  9. What did I do this week that matched what matters to me?
  10. How can I rest today without feeling guilty?

One line journal prompts of the day

For the days when you only have a minute. These pair well with a one line a day journal.

  1. Today in one sentence.
  2. The best part of today was...
  3. Today I noticed...
  4. Today I felt most like me when...
  5. Something I want to remember from today.
  6. Today I was annoyed by... and that is okay because...
  7. Today I am grateful for this person:
  8. One thing I would change about today.
  9. Something I ate today that I loved.
  10. Today's small win.
  11. A question I have tonight.
  12. Tomorrow I will try...

Tips for journaling every day

  • Set a tiny goal. Three sentences a day is enough. Small goals are easier to keep.
  • Pick a set time. Morning with coffee or night in bed both work. See our morning journal prompts and journal prompts before bed.
  • Do not break the chain twice. Missing one day is normal. Try not to miss two in a row.
  • Keep it in one place. A single notebook or our free online diary means you always know where to write.
  • Repeat favorite prompts. Answering the same question every Friday shows you how your weeks change over time.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a daily journal?

Write what happened, how you felt, and one thing you want to remember or do next. If that feels empty, answer a daily prompt instead. A theme for each day, like people on Tuesday or wins on Friday, makes it easier to start without staring at a blank page.

How long should daily journaling take?

Five to ten minutes is plenty for most people. Some days you will write one line, other days a full page. The habit matters more than the length. A short entry you write every day is more useful than a long one you only write once a month.

What is a good journal prompt of the day?

A good daily prompt is easy to answer in a few minutes and specific enough to spark a real memory. Try: What was the best ten minutes of today? What did I learn from someone today? What is today's small win? Pick from the lists above, or tap Write to answer one right now.

Is it okay to skip a day of journaling?

Yes. Skipping a day is normal and does not ruin the habit. Just come back the next day. Many people find it helps to have a few one line prompts ready for busy days, so the habit continues even when time is short.

Should I journal in the morning or at night?

Choose the time you can protect most days. Morning entries help you set intentions. Night entries help you look back and wind down. Some people do a quick line in the morning and a slightly longer entry at night.
