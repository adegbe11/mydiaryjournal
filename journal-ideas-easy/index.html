Easy journal ideas: 40 simple ideas you can do in 5 minutes

Easy journal ideas are ones you can finish in five minutes without thinking hard: write one sentence about your day, list three good things, color a mood square, or doodle what you ate. Here are 40 very simple ideas sorted by how much time and energy you have.

Why simple journal ideas work best

Most people quit journaling because it feels like homework. Simple ideas fix that. When a page takes two minutes, you do it again tomorrow, and a habit forms. You can always write more on days you feel like it.

The easiest rule: write the date and one line. If you do that, today counts.

Very simple journal ideas: one line or less

For days when you are tired or busy. Each one takes under a minute.

  • 1. One sentence about today. Just what happened. See the one line a day journal for a full system.
  • 2. Today in three words. For example: rainy, cozy, productive.
  • 3. Mood score. A number from 1 to 10 and one word for why.
  • 4. Best part of the day. One line, nothing more.
  • 5. One thing I am thankful for. Something specific, like the bus driver who waited.
  • 6. What I ate for dinner. Silly but fun to read back years later.
  • 7. Song of the day. The song stuck in your head and who sings it.
  • 8. Tomorrow I will. One small thing to do tomorrow. Writing it down lets you stop thinking about it tonight.
  • 9. A word I want to remember. A new word, a nickname, or something someone said.
  • 10. Weather and feeling. "Sunny outside, cloudy inside" counts.

Easy list ideas

Lists are easy because there is no need for full sentences. Each takes two to five minutes.

  • 11. Three good things. Three good things from today.
  • 12. Five things I can see. Look around and list them. A calm way to slow down.
  • 13. Things I finished. A done list, so you see what you got through.
  • 14. Snacks I love. Rank your top five.
  • 15. People I am glad I know. Write five names.
  • 16. Things I want to buy. Then wait a month and see which ones you still want.
  • 17. Places near me I have not tried. A cafe, a park, a library.
  • 18. Shows to watch next.
  • 19. Things that made me smile this week.
  • 20. Easy dinners I can make. Useful on busy nights.

Simple tracker ideas

Draw a small grid once a month. After that, filling it in takes seconds.

  • 21. Mood color. Color one box a day: green good, yellow okay, blue sad, red stressed. Our mood journal has more ideas.
  • 22. Water glasses. Eight tiny circles. Fill one per glass.
  • 23. Bedtime. Write the time you went to sleep.
  • 24. Steps or walks. A tick for every day you went outside.
  • 25. One habit. Pick just one, like reading ten pages, and tick each day you do it.
  • 26. Phone time. Write your daily screen time from your phone settings.
  • 27. Kind act. A tick each day you did one kind thing for someone.

Easy doodle and creative ideas

No art skill needed. Stick figures and simple shapes are perfect.

  • 28. Doodle your breakfast. Draw it, however badly.
  • 29. Weather doodle. A sun, cloud or raindrop next to the date.
  • 30. Emoji face. Draw the emoji that matches your day.
  • 31. Tape something in. A ticket, receipt, sticker or leaf, with the date.
  • 32. Six word story. Sum up today in exactly six words.
  • 33. Color the page. Fill a box with the color of your mood.
  • 34. Copy a line. Write one line from a book, song or friend that you liked. See journal ideas with quotes.

Easy ideas when you have ten minutes

  • 35. Brain dump. Write everything on your mind for five minutes without stopping.
  • 36. Letter to a friend. A short note you may or may not send.
  • 37. Answer one question. Pick a prompt from our journaling prompts for beginners.
  • 38. Week in five lines. Best, hardest, funniest, a lesson, next week.
  • 39. Morning plan. Top three tasks and one thing to enjoy.
  • 40. Before and after. How you felt at the start of the day and at the end.

That whole entry took under three minutes.

Not sure which idea to pick? Match it to how you feel right now. Each of these takes five minutes or less.

A simple weekly rotation

  • Monday: Tomorrow I will (8)
  • Tuesday: Three good things (11)
  • Wednesday: Mood color (21)
  • Thursday: Song of the day (7)
  • Friday: Things I finished (13)
  • Saturday: Tape something in (31)
  • Sunday: Week in five lines (38)

How to make journaling easy every day

  1. Pick a triggerJournal right after something you already do every day, like brushing your teeth or pouring coffee.
  2. Keep it in sightLeave the notebook open on your pillow or desk, or save our online journal to your phone home screen.
  3. Set the bar lowOne line is a full entry. Anything more is a bonus.
  4. Skip guiltMissed a week? Write today's date and carry on. No need to catch up.
  5. Rotate ideasCopy five ideas from this page to the inside cover and pick one each night.

Questions people ask

What is the easiest way to journal?

Write the date and one sentence about your day. That is it. If you have more time, add three good things or how you felt. Keeping it this small makes it easy to do every day, which matters more than writing a lot.

What should I write in my journal if I have nothing to write?

Use a simple list: three things you did, three things you can see, or one thing you are thankful for. You can also write "I do not know what to write" and keep going. A thought usually shows up within a minute.

Can journaling be just one sentence?

Yes. A one line a day journal is a popular way to journal. After a year you have 365 short memories, and reading them back is surprisingly moving.

What are simple journal ideas for beginners?

Three good things, a mood score, a done list, a doodle of your day, a song of the day, and a one line summary. Each takes under five minutes and needs no special supplies.

What time of day is best to journal?

Whenever you will actually do it. Mornings are good for planning and intentions. Evenings are good for reviewing the day and clearing your head before sleep. Try both for a week and see which sticks.
