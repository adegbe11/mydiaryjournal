September journal prompts: one for every day

September journal prompts help you settle into new routines as summer ends and fall begins. Here is a prompt for each of September's 30 days, plus extra sets for resetting your routines, the first day of fall and harvest season gratitude. Tap Write on any prompt to answer it in the diary above.

How to use these September journal prompts

For many people September feels more like a new year than January does. School starts, work picks up, and the air changes. Use the daily prompts to settle in, and use the routine reset set when your week feels out of control. The first day of fall set fits the week of the autumn equinox, around September 22.

30 September journal prompts: one for each day

Each number matches the date in September. Labor Day is the first Monday of the month, so use prompt 2 whenever it falls.

Days 1 to 7

  1. September often feels like a second new year. What do you want to begin this month?
  2. Labor Day honors working people. Write about the hardest working person you know.
  3. What is your relationship with work like right now? What would you change?
  4. Describe your morning routine as it is now, and as you wish it were.
  5. Which version of you showed up this summer: the rested one, the busy one, or someone new?
  6. Write about a grandparent or older person who shaped who you are.
  7. What is one thing you want to get organized this month?

Days 8 to 14

  1. Which smell tells you fall is coming?
  2. What is something you are learning right now, at school, at work or on your own?
  3. What is one boundary you need to set as life gets busier?
  4. September 11 is a day of remembrance in the US. Write about where you were, what you have learned about that day, or what helps you through hard anniversaries.
  5. What does a good Sunday evening look like when you are getting ready for the week?
  6. Write about an apple orchard, pumpkin patch or harvest festival you remember.
  7. What would make this fall the best one in years?

Days 15 to 21

  1. You are halfway through September. Which new routine is working, and which is not?
  2. Write about a time you started something late and still did well.
  3. What comfort food do you look forward to every fall?
  4. Describe the first morning this month when you needed a sweater.
  5. Who in your life needs encouragement right now? What could you say to them?
  6. What is one thing on your to do list that you could hand off or drop?
  7. Write about the colors you see outside right now. Have the leaves started to turn?

Days 22 to 28

  1. Fall begins around now. What do you want to let fall away, like leaves from a tree?
  2. What does home feel like when the weather cools?
  3. Write about a book you want to read this fall, and why now.
  4. What is one way you can make your evenings more restful?
  5. Describe your dream fall weekend getaway.
  6. What is something you used to dread about fall that you now enjoy?
  7. Write about a time you had to adjust to a big change.

Days 29 and 30

  1. What are three things you have done well this month?
  2. Look back on September. What new rhythm did you find?

Routine reset journal prompts

When the new season gets busy, these prompts help you plan a week that fits your life. For daily structure, see our daily journal prompts and morning journal prompts.

  1. What are the first three things you do after waking up? Do they help or hurt your day?
  2. Which part of your day feels most rushed? What could you prepare the night before?
  3. Write your ideal weekday schedule hour by hour, then circle one change you can make this week.
  4. What is one habit you dropped over the summer that you want back?
  5. How much time do you spend on your phone before bed? How do you feel about that?
  6. Which meals could you plan ahead to make busy nights easier?
  7. What does a realistic workout or movement routine look like for you this fall?
  8. What is one thing you could do every Sunday to feel ready for Monday?
  9. Where does your week leak time? Write down where the hours go.
  10. Describe how you want to feel at 9 p.m. on a weeknight.

First day of fall journal prompts

  1. What does fall mean to you? Write the first ten words that come to mind.
  2. Describe your favorite fall outfit, from the boots up.
  3. What is your favorite fall memory from childhood?
  4. Which fall activity do you look forward to most: leaf piles, football games, baking, or something else?
  5. Write about walking through crunchy leaves on a cool afternoon.
  6. What do you want to slow down and notice this autumn?
  7. Make a fall bucket list of ten things, big and small.
  8. What do the changing leaves remind you about change in your own life?
  9. Write about a warm drink and the place you most like to sip it.
  10. Describe the perfect fall day, from the first cup of coffee to the last light.
  11. Write a short poem about an apple, from the tree to the first bite.
  12. Plan a fall potluck. Who comes, and what does each person bring?

Harvest and gratitude journal ideas for September

Harvest season is a natural time to count what has grown. For more, see our gratitude journal prompts.

  1. What has grown in your life since last fall?
  2. Write about the people who have helped your year go well so far.
  3. What is a hard thing from this year that turned out to be useful?
  4. List ten simple things you are grateful for this week.
  5. Write about a meal made from fresh, local food that you loved.
  6. Which of your efforts this year is finally paying off?
  7. What is one thing you have more than enough of? How could you share it?
  8. Write a thank you note to someone who works hard so your life is easier, like a farmer, driver or cleaner.
  9. What strength has helped you most this year?
  10. What are you grateful you did not give up on?

More September journal ideas

  • Make a fall bucket list page and check things off as you go.
  • Track one new routine each day with a simple check mark.
  • Write a short entry every Sunday to plan the week ahead.
  • Press a leaf in a paper journal, or describe the first red leaf you find.
  • Starting fresh this fall? Try our journaling prompts for beginners.

Tips for journaling in September

  • Fit it to your new schedule. Pick a set writing time, like right after school drop-off or your lunch break.
  • Save the reset prompts for Sunday. They work best when you have half an hour to plan.
  • Read back. Look at your August pages and notice how your mood has changed.
  • Keep going in October. When September ends, continue with October journal prompts.

In the southern hemisphere, September is early spring. The spring sets in our March journal prompts may fit your season better.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in September?

September is about new routines and the start of fall. Write about how your days have changed since summer, what you want to begin, and the first signs of autumn around you. Labor Day, the start of school, apple season and the first day of fall all make good topics. End the month by noting the rhythm you found.

What are good fall journal prompts?

Good fall prompts help you notice the season and think about change. Write about your favorite fall memory, a walk through crunchy leaves, what the changing trees remind you of, and what you want to let go of before winter. A fall bucket list and a cozy drink prompt are easy places to start.

How can journaling help me build a new routine?

Writing helps you see where your time really goes. Start by describing your current day hour by hour, then write the day you want. Pick one change, try it for a week, and write a line each night about how it went. Seeing your progress on paper makes it easier to keep going.

Why does September feel like a fresh start?

For most of us, years of school trained us to see September as a beginning. New classes, new supplies and new schedules all start now. Work often picks up after summer, too. That makes September a good time to set goals, even if you did not set any in January.
