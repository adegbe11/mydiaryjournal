Guided journal: what it is, how to use it, and free prompts

A guided journal is a journal with prompts or questions already on each page, so you know exactly what to write. Instead of a blank page, you answer a question like "What made you smile today?" It is the easiest way to start journaling, and you can make your own for free with the prompts below.

What is a guided journal?

A guided journal gives you a structure to follow. Each page has a prompt, a few questions, or a simple layout to fill in. Some are themed, like gratitude, anxiety, faith, or self discovery. Others mix daily questions with weekly check-ins.

Compare that to a blank journal, where you decide everything yourself:

Guided journalBlank journal
What is on the pageA prompt or questionsEmpty lines
Best forBeginners, busy people, anyone who gets stuckPeople who already know what they want to write
FreedomLess, but more focusComplete
Time per entryOften 5 minutesVaries

Many people start with a guided journal, then move to a blank one once the habit is strong. Others stay with guided pages for years because they like the focus.

Types of guided journals

  • Daily guided journal: the same few questions every day, like mood, gratitude and one goal. See our daily journal.
  • Gratitude journal: asks for good things each day. See our gratitude journal.
  • Self discovery journal: deep questions about your values, past and dreams.
  • Mental health journal: gentle check-ins about feelings and self-care.
  • Prayer or Bible journal: a verse, reflection questions and a prayer. See prayer journal and Bible journal.
  • Five year or line a day journal: one short question per day, repeated each year. See one line a day journal.

Is a guided journal right for you?

A guided journal suits some people better than others. Here is an honest look.

A guided journal is a great fit if you

  • Freeze in front of a blank page
  • Have tried journaling before and quit
  • Only have five minutes a day
  • Want help with one area, like gratitude, anxiety, faith or goals
  • Like answering questions more than writing freely

A blank journal may suit you better if you

  • Already know what you want to write
  • Find set questions boring or limiting
  • Need space for whatever is on your mind today, not what the page asks

Many people keep both: a guided page on busy days and free writing when there is more to say. If a prompt does not fit your day, write what you need to instead. The guide is there to help, not to box you in.

How to use a guided journal

  1. Pick a themeChoose what you want help with: calm, gratitude, goals, faith, or knowing yourself better.
  2. Answer one prompt at a timeRead the question, take a breath, and write the first honest answer that comes. Do not overthink.
  3. Skip prompts that do not fitYou do not have to answer every question. Come back to hard ones later or not at all.
  4. Write a little past the questionIf an answer leads somewhere else, follow it. The prompt is a starting point, not a box.
  5. Keep a set timeFive minutes with your morning drink or before bed is enough to build the habit.

40 guided journal prompts

Use these to make your own guided journal. Pick one a day, or choose a section and work through it for a week.

Daily check-in

  1. How am I feeling right now, in one word? Why that word?
  2. What is the one thing I want to get done today?
  3. What is something I am looking forward to this week?
  4. What drained my energy today, and what gave me energy?
  5. What did I learn today, even something small?
  6. What is one thing I want to let go of before bed?
  7. How did I take care of myself today?
  8. What would make tomorrow a little better?

Gratitude

  1. Who made my life easier this week, and how?
  2. What is a small comfort I enjoyed today?
  3. What is something about my body I am thankful for?
  4. What place makes me feel at home?
  5. What challenge am I grateful for now that it is over?
  6. What simple thing would I miss most if it were gone?
  7. Who taught me something important?
  8. What made me laugh recently?

Getting to know yourself

  1. What are three words my close friends would use to describe me?
  2. When do I feel most like myself?
  3. What did I love doing as a child that I have stopped?
  4. What is a belief I have changed my mind about?
  5. What am I proud of that nobody knows about?
  6. What drains me that I keep saying yes to?
  7. What does a good life look like to me?
  8. What do I want people to feel after spending time with me?

Goals and growth

  1. What is one goal I keep putting off, and what is the first tiny step?
  2. What would I try if I were not afraid of looking silly?
  3. What habit would change my life most if I kept it for a year?
  4. What did I do this month that past me would be proud of?
  5. Where do I want to be one year from today?
  6. What is one skill I want to learn, and why?
  7. What is getting in my way right now?
  8. Who could help me with a goal I have?

Calm and feelings

  1. What is on my mind that I have not said out loud?
  2. What can I control today, and what can I let go of?
  3. What would I say to a friend who felt the way I feel now?
  4. Where in my body am I holding stress?
  5. What helps me feel safe and calm?
  6. What is a worry I can put down for tonight?
  7. What is one kind thing I can do for myself this week?
  8. What went better than I expected recently?

What is inside a guided journal

Guided journals you buy, and the ones you make yourself, usually include some of these parts:

PartWhat it doesExample
Daily promptGives each page a focus"What drained me today?"
Check-in boxesQuick mood, sleep or energy ratingMood: 1 2 3 4 5
Gratitude linesBuilds a habit of noticing good thingsThree numbered lines
TrackersShows habits over timeWater, steps, reading, prayer
Weekly reviewLooks back and plans ahead"Best moment this week"
Quotes or versesSomething to think aboutA short line at the top
Free spaceRoom for anything elseA blank box or a few lines

When you choose or make a guided journal, look for prompts that sound like questions you would really want to answer. Avoid ones that feel preachy or too long. Short pages you finish beat long pages you dread.

Make your own free guided journal

You do not need to buy a guided journal. You can make one in a few minutes:

  1. Choose five to eight prompts from the list above that you want to answer often.
  2. Write them at the top of each page in a notebook, or keep them in a note you read before writing.
  3. Add a date line and a mood rating.
  4. Once a week, add one deeper prompt from the self discovery section.

Questions people ask

What is a guided journal?

A guided journal is a journal with prompts, questions or simple layouts on each page that tell you what to write about. It removes the blank page problem and gives each entry a focus. Guided journals come in many themes, including gratitude, mental health, faith and self discovery.

Are guided journals good for beginners?

Yes. Guided journals are one of the easiest ways to start because you always know what to write. A single question is less scary than an empty page. Many beginners use guided prompts for a few months, then try free writing once the habit feels natural.

What is the difference between a guided journal and a regular journal?

A regular journal has blank or lined pages and you decide what to write. A guided journal gives you prompts or a structure on each page. Guided journals offer focus and ease. Regular journals offer total freedom.

How do you use a guided journal?

Pick a time each day, read the prompt, and write your honest answer. Skip prompts that do not fit you. If your answer leads to new thoughts, keep writing. Five to ten minutes a day is enough. Reread older pages now and then to see how you have changed.

Can I make my own guided journal?

Yes. Choose a handful of prompts you like, write them at the top of each page in any notebook, and add a date line. You can also use a free online journal and paste a prompt at the top of each entry.

What should I look for in a guided journal?

Look for a theme that matches what you want, such as gratitude, mental health, faith or goals. Check that the prompts feel natural and that the pages are short enough to finish in a few minutes. Some space for free writing is helpful. If you are not sure, try a free guided journal first by using a list of prompts.
