75 shadow work journal prompts for beginners

Shadow work journal prompts are questions that help you notice the parts of yourself you usually hide or push away, like anger, envy or shame, so you can understand them instead of fighting them. Below is a plain guide to what shadow work is and 75 gentle prompts for beginners. Write your answers privately in the diary at the top of this page.

What is shadow work?

The idea comes from Carl Jung, a Swiss psychiatrist. He used the word shadow for the parts of ourselves we do not like to admit to, often because we learned early on that they were not okay. Things like jealousy, anger, neediness, selfishness or even big dreams we were told were silly.

Those parts do not go away when we hide them. They tend to show up sideways: a strong reaction to someone that seems bigger than the moment, a habit we cannot explain, or the same argument with different people.

Shadow work simply means looking at those hidden parts with curiosity. It is not mystical and it is not about being a bad person. It is about understanding yourself more fully so those reactions have less control over you.

What is a shadow work journal?

A shadow work journal is a private place to answer these questions. Writing slows you down and lets you be honest in a way that is hard to do in conversation.

How to start shadow work journaling as a beginner

  1. Start light.Begin with the first two lists below. They are about noticing, not digging.
  2. One or two prompts per session.Shadow work can stir things up. Less is more.
  3. Write without judging.If an answer makes you cringe, write it anyway. Nobody is grading you.
  4. Notice resistance.If you want to skip a question, ask yourself why. That is often where the useful part is.
  5. Close gently.End each session with something kind: a walk, a cup of tea, or a line from our self-love journal prompts.

Shadow work can bring up painful memories. Journaling is not a replacement for professional care. If you have a history of trauma, consider doing this work with a therapist. If you feel overwhelmed or unsafe, stop and reach out for help. In the US, call or text 988, or contact local emergency services.

Journal questions for shadow work: noticing triggers

A trigger is a strong reaction that seems bigger than the situation. It is one of the clearest windows into your shadow.

  1. When did I last overreact to something small? What was really going on?
  2. Which kind of person annoys me almost instantly? What do they do?
  3. What comment, if someone said it about me, would sting the most?
  4. What do I get defensive about, even when nobody is attacking me?
  5. Which situations make me want to leave the room right away?
  6. What topic do I avoid bringing up with family?
  7. When I feel criticized, what is my first reaction: fight, freeze, hide or please?
  8. Which emotion am I most uncomfortable seeing in other people?
  9. Is there a behavior I judge in others that I sometimes do myself?
  10. What made me irritated today, and how long did it stay with me?

Shadow work prompts about envy and comparison

Envy is uncomfortable, but it often points straight to something you want and have not let yourself go after.

  1. Who do I feel jealous of right now? What exactly do they have?
  2. What does that envy tell me about something I want for myself?
  3. Whose success do I struggle to celebrate? Why?
  4. Which trait in other people do I secretly admire but would never claim for myself?
  5. Where do I compare myself to others most: looks, money, career, family?
  6. If I let myself want something big without apologizing, what would it be?
  7. What permission am I waiting for that someone else seems to already have?
  8. How do I act when someone gets something I wanted?
  9. Which compliment do I give others that I rarely give myself?
  10. What would change if I treated envy as a clue instead of a flaw?

Shadow work prompts about anger and hard feelings

  1. How was anger handled in my home growing up?
  2. What do I do with my anger: say it, swallow it or let it leak out?
  3. When did I last feel truly angry? What was underneath it: hurt, fear, unfairness?
  4. Which feeling do I hide most from the people closest to me?
  5. What do I feel ashamed of that I have never told anyone?
  6. When do I go cold or shut down instead of saying what I feel?
  7. Is there a resentment I keep feeding? What would it take to let it rest?
  8. Which feelings was I told were too much as a child?
  9. How do I act when I feel left out?
  10. What would my anger say if it could speak calmly for one minute?

Shadow work journal prompts about patterns

  1. What argument do I seem to have over and over, even with different people?
  2. Which habit do I keep promising to stop but never do? What does it give me?
  3. How do I sabotage things just when they start going well?
  4. What role do I fall into in groups: the fixer, the joker, the quiet one, the boss?
  5. Which kind of relationship do I keep ending up in?
  6. How do I act when I feel I am not good enough?
  7. What do I do to make people like me that I do not really want to do?
  8. Where do I play small so I do not stand out?
  9. Which excuse do I use most often?
  10. What pattern from my parents do I see in myself, good or bad?

Shadow work prompts about childhood and the past

Many shadow parts started in childhood as ways to stay safe or loved. For a gentler take, try inner child journal prompts.

  1. What part of me did I hide as a kid to fit in or be loved?
  2. What did I get in trouble for as a child that was actually just normal?
  3. Which family rule was never said out loud but everyone followed?
  4. What label was I given growing up: the smart one, the difficult one, the good one?
  5. How did I get attention as a child? Do I still do that?
  6. What did I learn about money, love or success by watching the adults around me?
  7. Which childhood fear do I still carry in a grown-up form?
  8. What did I need to hear as a child that I still find hard to believe?
  9. Which memory do I tend to skip over when telling my life story?
  10. What would my younger self want me to stop pretending about?

Shadow work prompts about relationships

Other people often act like mirrors. What we notice most in them can say a lot about us.

  1. What do I expect from others that I do not give myself?
  2. Which of my partner's or friend's habits bothers me most? Do I have a version of it?
  3. How do I act when someone pulls away from me?
  4. What do I keep score of in my relationships?
  5. When do I stay quiet to avoid conflict, and what does that cost me?
  6. What do I need from people that I feel embarrassed to need?
  7. Which person from my past still takes up room in my head? Why?
  8. How do I behave when I feel someone has more power than me?
  9. What do I tend to blame others for that might partly be mine?
  10. What kind of love do I find hard to accept?

Prompts for acceptance and integration

The goal of shadow work is not to get rid of these parts. It is to understand them, so you can choose how to act instead of reacting. This is what people mean by integration.

  1. Pick one hidden trait from today's writing. How might it be helping me?
  2. What would a healthy version of my anger, envy or stubbornness look like?
  3. Write a short note of thanks to a part of me I have been ashamed of.
  4. Which of my so-called flaws is also a strength in the right place?
  5. What do I want to do differently the next time I feel triggered?
  6. How would I treat a friend who told me what I just wrote?
  7. What part of myself am I ready to stop hiding from the people I trust?
  8. What feels lighter after writing today?
  9. What do I understand about myself now that I did not this morning?
  10. How can I be patient with myself while I keep learning?
  11. Write a sentence that accepts both my light and my shadow side.
  12. What does a whole, honest version of me look like?
  13. Which prompt on this page do I most want to come back to in a month?
  14. What is one small action this week that comes from understanding, not shame?
  15. How has my view of other people softened since I started looking at myself?

Tips for safe shadow work journaling

  • Go slow. One honest page is worth more than ten rushed ones.
  • Watch your body. If you feel shaky, dizzy or flooded, stop and ground yourself. Name five things you can see.
  • Do not use it to beat yourself up. The point is understanding, not proof that you are bad.
  • Look for patterns over weeks. Reread entries now and then. The same theme often shows up again and again.
  • Keep it private. This diary stays in your browser on this device, with no account. The PIN lock helps on a shared computer.
  • Get support for deep wounds. A therapist can help with what a journal cannot. See therapy journal prompts.

Questions people ask

What is shadow work in simple terms?

Shadow work means looking honestly at the parts of yourself you usually hide or deny, like jealousy, anger or shame. The idea comes from psychiatrist Carl Jung, who called these hidden parts the shadow. By noticing and understanding them, you can react less and choose your actions more freely. It is self-reflection, not anything mystical.

How do I start shadow work as a beginner?

Start by noticing your triggers: moments when you react more strongly than the situation calls for. Write about one at a time using a prompt. Keep sessions short, one or two prompts, and end with something calming. Move to childhood and pattern questions only once you feel steady.

Is shadow work safe?

For most people, gentle shadow work journaling is a safe way to reflect. It can stir up strong feelings or painful memories, though. If you have a history of trauma, or if writing leaves you overwhelmed, work with a therapist. Stop any time you feel unsafe, and reach out for help if you are in crisis.

How often should I do shadow work?

Once or twice a week is plenty for most people. Shadow work is deeper than a daily check-in, so it helps to give yourself time to process between sessions. On other days, lighter journaling like gratitude journal prompts can balance it out.

What is the difference between shadow work and inner child work?

They overlap. Inner child work focuses on caring for the child you were and meeting needs that were not met. Shadow work focuses on traits and feelings you push away, which often started in childhood. Many people move back and forth between the two.
