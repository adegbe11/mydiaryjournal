Diary vs journal: what is the difference?

A diary is a daily record of what happened. A journal is a place to think on paper about your feelings, ideas and goals, and you do not have to write in it every day. Many people mix the two, and that is fine.

The short answer

Both words come from the idea of a day. Diary comes from the Latin word for a daily allowance, and journal comes from the French word for a day. Over time the two words drifted apart in how people use them.

Today most people use diary for a dated, private record of daily events and journal for any notebook where you reflect, plan or create. A diary answers "what happened?" A journal answers "what do I think about it?"

There is no rule that says you must pick one. A diary entry can end with a few lines of reflection, and that is how most people really write.

What a diary entry looks like

A diary entry starts with the date and tells the day in order. It is often written to the diary itself, which is where Dear diary comes from.

Notice how it is mostly facts. Years from now, a page like this helps you remember where you were and who was in your life. See more in our diary entry examples.

What a journal entry looks like

A journal entry usually starts with a question or a feeling and goes deeper. It may not mention the day at all.

This is reflection. It turns an event into something you learn from. If you are not sure what to write, start with journal prompts.

Which one should you keep?

Keep a diary if you want to

  • Remember your days, trips and the people you met
  • Build a simple daily writing habit
  • Track things over time, like sleep, moods or meals
  • Have a private place to vent after a hard day

Keep a journal if you want to

  • Understand your feelings and patterns
  • Work through a decision or a problem
  • Practice gratitude, prayer or goal setting
  • Write when you need it, not on a schedule

If you cannot decide, do both on one page: two or three lines about the day, then one question to think about. This is sometimes called a diary journal.

How to start today

  1. Pick where you will writeA paper notebook, a notes app or a private online diary. The best one is the one you will open again tomorrow.
  2. Write the dateEven in a journal, a date helps you see how you have changed when you read back.
  3. Start smallThree sentences is enough. Write what happened, how you felt, and one thing you want to remember.
  4. Pick a regular timeMany people write at night before bed. Others prefer the morning with coffee. Tie it to something you already do.

Questions people ask

Is a diary the same as a journal?

They are close but not the same. A diary is usually a dated record of daily events. A journal is a broader word for any notebook where you reflect, plan or create. In everyday speech many people use the words interchangeably.

Is it better to have a diary or a journal?

Neither is better. A diary is great for remembering your life and building a daily habit. A journal is better for working through feelings, ideas and goals. Many people combine them on the same page.

Do you have to write in a diary every day?

No. A diary is traditionally daily, but skipping days is normal. Writing a few lines most days is better than long entries once a month.

Why do people say dear diary?

Writing to the diary as if it were a friend makes it easier to be honest. It became popular through published diaries and children's books, and many people still start entries that way.

Is a planner a diary or a journal?

A planner looks forward and holds plans, appointments and to do lists. A diary looks back at what happened. A journal can do either. Bullet journals mix planning and journaling in one notebook.
