How to journal

How to start a journal (and actually keep going)

To start a journal, pick a place to write, choose a regular time, and write a short first entry today. Start each entry with the date and one honest sentence about how you feel. Keep it to five minutes at first so the habit is easy to keep.

How to start a journal in 7 steps

  1. Decide why you want to journalTo remember your days, calm your mind, track goals, pray, or get to know yourself. Your reason shapes what you write. You can have more than one, and it can change as you go.
  2. Pick where you will writeA paper notebook, a notes app, or a private online journal. Choose the one you will actually open. A cheap notebook you use beats a beautiful one you are afraid to ruin.
  3. Choose a time and tie it to a habitWrite after brushing your teeth, with your morning coffee, or on the bus home. Linking journaling to something you already do makes it automatic.
  4. Write your first pageDate it, write why you are starting, and describe your life right now. See the first page ideas below.
  5. Use a simple structure for each entryWhat happened, how you felt, what you think about it. Or use a prompt. Structure stops the blank page from winning.
  6. Keep it short for two weeksFive minutes or three sentences. Small wins build the habit. Write more only when you want to.
  7. Read back once a monthLook at old entries. You will notice patterns, progress and memories you had forgotten. This is what keeps most people going.

How to start a journal: first page ideas

The first page feels like a big deal, but it does not need to be. Here are easy ways to begin:

  • A hello note. Your name, the date, and why you are starting.
  • A snapshot of now. Your age, where you live, who you live with, what you do all day, your current favorite song.
  • A letter to future you. What do you hope will be different when you read this in a year?
  • Your rules. For example: "No spelling checks. Honesty only. One line counts."
  • Three goals. What you hope journaling helps with.
  • A list of 10 things you love right now. Easy and fun to read later.

Some people leave the first page blank and come back to it later. That is allowed too.

How to start a journal entry

Every time you sit down, the first line is the hardest. Use one of these openers to get moving:

  • Today I...
  • Right now I feel... because...
  • The thing on my mind is...
  • Something I noticed today...
  • I am grateful for...
  • I keep thinking about...
  • If today had a title, it would be...
  • Dear diary, you will not believe...

Write the date at the top every time. Even if you skip days, dates let you see how you have changed when you read back.

Pick a type of journal to start with

If a general journal feels too open, start with one focused type. You can always mix later.

  • Gratitude journal: three good things a day. Great for beginners.
  • Daily diary: what happened, in order. Good for memory keepers.
  • Mood journal: a rating and a reason. Good for spotting patterns.
  • Dream journal: written right after waking up.
  • Prayer journal: thanks, requests and answered prayers.
  • Guided journal: a prompt for every page, so you never start from nothing.

A 14 day plan to start a journal

If you like a clear plan, follow this for two weeks. Each day takes five minutes or less. By the end, you will have tried the most common styles and you will know which one fits you.

DayWhat to write
1Your first page: why you are starting and a snapshot of your life now
2Three good things from today
3Your day in three sentences: morning, afternoon, evening
4Your mood from 1 to 10, and why
5Something on your mind that you have not said out loud
6A list of ten things you love right now
7Read back over days 1 to 6. What did you like writing most?
8A letter to someone you miss
9One goal for this month and the first small step
10Free write for five minutes without stopping
11A worry, then the most likely outcome
12A favorite memory from childhood
13What you want more of and less of in your life
14Read back over two weeks. Write what you noticed and what you will keep doing

After day 14, keep the style you enjoyed most, or keep rotating. Either way, you now have a habit and two weeks of pages to look back on.

What you need to start (not much)

You do not need special supplies. Here is all it takes:

  • Something to write in. Any notebook, loose paper, a notes app, or an online diary.
  • Something to write with. A pen you like makes it a little more pleasant, but any pen works.
  • Five quiet minutes. In bed, in the car before you walk in, or at the table after dinner.
  • A place to keep it private. A drawer, a bag, or a PIN lock if you write online.
  • A few prompts for stuck days. Save a short list where you will see it.

That is it. Pretty notebooks, stickers and colored pens are fun, but they are optional. Many people wait months for the perfect setup and never start. A plain notebook you write in today is better than a perfect one you buy next month.

Be honest in your journal, including about hard things. It is your private space, so you do not need to sound wise or upbeat. Write as if you are talking to someone you trust completely.

How to keep the habit going

  • Lower the bar on bad days. One line is a win. "Tired. Pizza. Bed." counts.
  • Never try to catch up. If you miss a week, write today. Do not try to fill in every missed day.
  • Keep your journal where you will see it. On your pillow, next to the kettle, or as a home screen shortcut.
  • Use prompts when you are bored. Our journaling prompts for beginners are short and easy.
  • Make it pleasant. A nice pen, a cup of tea, a quiet corner. Small rewards help.

It also helps to know what to expect. The first few days often feel awkward, like talking to yourself. That is normal. Around the second week, many people notice they look forward to it, or that their thoughts feel less crowded. If you still dread it after a few weeks, change the method rather than quitting. Try a different time of day, a different place, or a different style, like lists or daily prompts.

Most people quit because their entries got too long and started to feel like work. If that happens, go back to three sentences.

Questions people ask

How do I start journaling if I have never done it?

Get any notebook or open a free online journal, write today's date, and answer one question: how do I feel right now and why? Keep it to five minutes. Do this at the same time each day for two weeks. Once it feels normal, you can try prompts, lists or longer entries.

What is the best way to start a journal entry?

Write the date, then start with a simple opener like "Today I..." or "Right now I feel...". Finish the sentence honestly and keep going. If nothing comes, describe what is around you or answer one prompt. Getting the first line down is the hardest part.

What should I write on the first page of my journal?

Write your name, the date, and why you are starting. Many people add a snapshot of their life right now, a few goals, or a letter to their future self. The first page does not have to be perfect. You can even leave it blank and come back.

How often should I journal?

Daily is great for building a habit, but three times a week works well for many people. What matters most is coming back regularly. Short, frequent entries tend to help more than long ones once in a while.

Should I journal in the morning or at night?

Either works. Morning journaling helps you plan and set a mood for the day. Night journaling helps you process the day and clear your head before sleep. Try both for a week and keep the one you actually stick with.

How do I start a journal when I don't know what to write?

Start with a gratitude list or the date and one sentence about how you feel. Prompts help a lot when you are new, so keep a short list nearby. You can also describe what is in front of you right now. Once the pen is moving, the next thought usually comes. Short entries are completely fine.
