June journal prompts: a prompt for every day

June journal prompts help you slow down as summer begins and take stock of the first half of the year. Here is a prompt for each of June's 30 days, plus extra sets for Father's Day, the summer solstice and a midyear check-in. Tap Write on any prompt and answer it in the diary above.

How to use these June journal prompts

June days are long, so try writing in the evening while it is still light. Answer today's prompt or pick one that fits your mood. Save the midyear check-in for the last weekend of the month, when the first half of the year is almost done. If Father's Day brings up mixed feelings, those prompts make room for that too.

30 June journal prompts: one for each day

Each number matches the date in June. Father's Day is the third Sunday, so use those prompts that weekend.

Days 1 to 7

  1. Summer is starting. What three words do you want to describe this summer?
  2. Write about your favorite summer as a child. What made it special?
  3. What does a perfect summer evening look like to you?
  4. Describe your first swim of the year in a lake, pool or ocean, or the last swim you remember.
  5. What is something you want to do this summer that costs nothing?
  6. What does freedom feel like in your everyday life?
  7. Write about a wedding you went to and the moment you remember most.

Days 8 to 14

  1. What are you reading this summer, or what do you want to read?
  2. Which fruit tastes most like summer to you? Write about where you ate it.
  3. What is a skill you want to practice while the days are long?
  4. Write about a road trip you took or would like to take.
  5. Who do you want to spend more time with this summer?
  6. What is one thing your dad, or a father figure, taught you that you still use?
  7. Describe the sounds of summer where you live.

Days 15 to 21

  1. You are halfway through June. How are you actually spending your free time?
  2. What does being a good father mean to you?
  3. Write about a time an older man in your life showed you kindness.
  4. What is one summer tradition you want to start this year?
  5. Juneteenth marks the end of slavery in the United States. Write about what freedom and justice mean to you today.
  6. This is one of the longest days of the year. How did you spend the extra light?
  7. Summer officially begins around now. What would make this season feel well spent?

Days 22 to 28

  1. What is the best thing about having nowhere to be?
  2. Write about a summer job you had, or the first job you ever had.
  3. What is something you would like to learn about the place where you live?
  4. Describe the best ice cream, lemonade or cold treat you have ever had on a hot day.
  5. What did you love doing when school let out as a kid?
  6. What is one thing you are glad you started this year?
  7. Write about a night you stayed up late and why it was worth it.

Days 29 and 30

  1. What would you like the second half of the year to feel like?
  2. Today ends the first half of the year. Write a short letter to yourself about the last six months.

Father's Day journal prompts

For dads, for children, and for anyone who misses a father or never had one. Dads who pray may like our prayer journal for men.

  1. Describe your dad, or the man who raised you, in a single scene you remember clearly.
  2. What did your father do on weekends when you were young?
  3. Which of his habits do you have now, for better or worse?
  4. What is a question you have always wanted to ask your father?
  5. If you are a dad, what moment made you feel most like a father?
  6. If you are a dad, what kind of father do you hope your kids describe one day?
  7. If your relationship with your father is hard, write about what you needed from him and where you have found it since.
  8. Write about a grandfather, uncle, coach or neighbor who filled a father's role for you.
  9. What is the best advice a man in your life ever gave you?
  10. Plan a simple way to honor your dad or a father figure this weekend.

Summer solstice and long day journal ideas

  1. Watch the sunset tonight and write about it before the sky gets dark.
  2. What do you want to grow during the brightest part of the year?
  3. Write about a time you felt completely carefree.
  4. What is one thing you would do if the sun never set tonight?
  5. How does your energy change in summer? Do you feel more awake, or more tired?
  6. What lights you up these days: people, places or projects?
  7. Write about fireflies, stars, or anything that glows on a summer night.
  8. Where is your favorite place to sit outside in the evening?
  9. What does summer give you that no other season does?
  10. Write a list of 20 small summer pleasures.
  11. What is your favorite way to cool down on a hot afternoon?
  12. Write about a summer camp, sleepover or family reunion you still think about.

Midyear check-in prompts

Six months are done and six are left. Compare your answers with what you wrote using our new year journal prompts back in January.

  1. What goals did you set in January? Which ones are still on track?
  2. What has happened this year that you did not see coming?
  3. Which goal do you want to drop, and which do you want to give more effort?
  4. What are you most grateful for from the first half of the year?
  5. What drained you most in the last six months, and can you change it?
  6. What habit has made the biggest positive difference this year?
  7. Write about a moment this year when you surprised yourself.
  8. What would you like to finish by December?
  9. Rate the first half of the year from 1 to 10. What would make the second half a 10?
  10. Write three short promises to yourself for July through December.

More June journal ideas

  • Keep a summer journal with one photo and one sentence each day.
  • Make a summer reading list in a reading journal.
  • Write down the funny things your kids say this summer. Those lines are easy to forget.
  • Start a travel journal for any trip, even a day trip.
  • List every new food you try this summer.

Tips for journaling in June

  • Write outside if you can. A porch, a park bench or a balcony makes the habit feel like a treat.
  • Keep it light on busy days. One line about the best moment is enough.
  • Plan the second half. Use the last days of June for the midyear check-in, then set two goals for July.
  • Keep going in July. When June ends, continue with July journal prompts.

In the southern hemisphere, June is the start of winter. The holiday and cozy prompts in our December journal prompts may fit your season better.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in June?

June is the start of summer and the end of the first half of the year. Write about summer plans, long evenings, and the people you want to see more. Father's Day, Juneteenth and the summer solstice give you natural themes. In the last week, look back at your year so far and reset your goals.

What should I write for Father's Day?

Write a clear memory of your dad or a father figure, a habit you got from him, and a question you have always wanted to ask. If you are a dad, write about the moment you first felt like one. If the day is hard, write about what you needed and where you have found it since.

What is a midyear review in a journal?

A midyear review is a short check-in at the end of June. You look at the goals you set in January, note what is working and what is not, and decide what to keep, change or drop. It takes about twenty minutes and helps the second half of the year feel more focused.

How do I keep journaling during summer?

Lower the bar. Summer routines are loose, so aim for one line a day instead of a full page. Keep your diary handy on your phone or by your bed, write while you wait, and use the themed prompts on rainy days. A few short notes are better than nothing when you look back later.
