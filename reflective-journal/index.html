Reflective journal: what it is and how to write one

A reflective journal is a place where you write about an experience and then think about what it means, what you learned, and what you will do next. It goes beyond "what happened" to "why it mattered." People use reflective journals for school, work, training and personal growth.

Reflective journal meaning

To reflect means to look back at something and think about it carefully. A reflective journal is simply a record of that thinking, written down.

A regular diary says "I gave a talk today." A reflective journal says "I gave a talk today. I rushed the middle because I was nervous. Next time I will slow down and pause after each point." The second version turns an event into a lesson.

Reflective journals are used in many places. Nursing, teaching and social work students often keep one during training. Teachers ask pupils to reflect after labs, books and projects. Many workplaces use reflection to learn from projects and mistakes. And plenty of people keep a reflective journal at home simply to learn from their own lives and stop repeating the same patterns.

Reflective writing does not need to be formal. It can be a few lines after a hard conversation or a long entry after a big week. What makes it reflective is the question you ask yourself: not only "what happened?" but "what does this teach me?"

How to write a reflective journal

  1. Pick one experienceA class, a conversation, a mistake, a win, a book, a hard day. One is enough.
  2. Describe it brieflyTwo or three sentences of facts. Who, what, where. Keep this part short.
  3. Write how you feltBefore, during and after. Feelings often show what mattered most.
  4. Ask whyWhy did it go the way it did? What did you do well? What could have gone better?
  5. Connect itLink it to something you know: a past experience, a class idea, a value you hold.
  6. Decide what is nextOne thing you will do the same or differently next time.

Spend most of your words on steps 3 to 6. Description is the least important part of reflective writing.

Two reflective writing models, explained simply

What? So what? Now what?

The easiest model. Three questions: What happened? So what does it mean or why does it matter? Now what will I do about it? It works for quick daily reflections and short class entries.

The Gibbs reflective cycle

Created by Graham Gibbs in 1988 and used widely in nursing, teaching and other courses. It walks you through six stages:

  1. Description: what happened?
  2. Feelings: what were you thinking and feeling?
  3. Evaluation: what was good and bad about it?
  4. Analysis: why did it happen that way? What helps explain it?
  5. Conclusion: what did you learn? What else could you have done?
  6. Action plan: what will you do if it happens again?

Descriptive vs reflective writing

The most common problem in reflective journals, especially graded ones, is too much description and too little reflection. Here is the same moment written both ways.

The second entry picks one moment, explains why it happened, links it to a reading, and ends with a change. Use these words to move from describing to reflecting: because, I realized, this suggests, this connects to, next time.

A third model: Kolb's learning cycle

Some courses use David Kolb's experiential learning cycle. It has four parts: you have an experience, you reflect on it, you draw a general lesson from it, then you try that lesson in a new situation. It is a lot like What? So what? Now what?, with an extra step of testing what you learned.

Reflective journals for school, training and work

In courses with a placement or practical part, such as nursing, teaching or social work, a reflective journal is often graded. It also works as a record of how your thinking changes over the term. If yours will be read by a tutor or supervisor, keep a few things in mind:

  • Remember your reader. A graded journal is not fully private. Be honest, but professional.
  • Protect privacy. Use initials or roles, never full names of patients, clients or children.
  • Show change over time. Refer back to earlier entries: "In week 2 I avoided this. Now I..."
  • Use evidence. Link your points to readings, lectures, or feedback from your supervisor.
  • Follow the format. Some courses want a set model, length or headings. Check the guide first.

There is no single correct format outside of class. A reflective journal can be a notebook, a typed document, a blog, voice notes or even a scrapbook. What makes it reflective is the thinking, not the format.

Reflective journal writing tips

  • Be honest, not harsh. Reflection is about learning, not beating yourself up.
  • Look at good moments too. Ask what went right and how to repeat it.
  • Use "I". It is your experience. Write in the first person.
  • Write soon. Details and feelings fade quickly.
  • Reread monthly. Look for lessons that keep coming up. Those are your biggest areas for growth.
  • Use prompts when stuck. Try our reflective journal prompts or journal prompts for self growth.

Questions to guide your reflection

  • What surprised me?
  • What did I do well, and why did it work?
  • What would I do differently?
  • What did this teach me about myself?
  • What assumption did I have that turned out to be wrong?
  • How does this connect to something I already know?
  • What is one small change I can make this week?

Questions people ask

What is a reflective journal?

A reflective journal is a written record where you describe experiences and think about what they mean. It includes what happened, how you felt, what you learned, and what you will do next. It is used in school, training, work and personal growth to turn experience into learning.

What should be included in a reflective journal?

Include a short description of the experience, your thoughts and feelings, an honest look at what went well and what did not, why it happened that way, what you learned, and an action plan for next time. Models like Gibbs or What? So what? Now what? help cover each part.

How do you start a reflective journal entry?

Start with the date and a one or two sentence description of the experience. Then move straight to how you felt or what stood out. Openers like "What surprised me was" or "Looking back, I realize" help you shift from describing to reflecting.

What is the difference between a reflective journal and a diary?

A diary mainly records what happened. A reflective journal focuses on what you think about it and what you learned. A diary entry may end after the events. A reflective entry usually ends with a lesson or a plan.

How often should you write a reflective journal?

For a course, follow the assignment, often weekly. For personal growth, two or three times a week works well, or right after any important experience. Short, regular reflection helps more than a long entry once a month.

What are the benefits of keeping a reflective journal?

A reflective journal can help you learn from experience instead of repeating mistakes, notice patterns in how you think and act, and see how your thinking changes over time. Many people also find it helps them process feelings and stress. In school and training, it shows your teacher how you are learning, not just what you did.
