Diary entry about a rainy day (5 examples)

A diary entry about a rainy day gives the date and day, starts with Dear Diary, describes the rain with your senses (sound, smell, sight, touch), tells what you did, and says how the weather made you feel. Below are 5 complete model entries, from a short one for young kids to longer ones for older students, plus rain words, steps and a page to write your own.

Rainy day diary entry format

A rainy day entry uses the normal diary layout. What makes it special is description. Examiners love to see that you can paint the weather with words, not just say "it rained".

  • Date, day and time at the top, for example: Saturday, 12 July, 7 p.m.
  • Dear Diary, on its own line.
  • Opening: what the sky and the rain were like when the day began.
  • Middle: what you did because of the rain, written in the past tense.
  • Feelings: cozy, gloomy, playful, bored, peaceful. Say why.
  • Closing and name, such as Good night, then your name.

Try to use at least three senses. What did the rain sound like on the roof? What did the air smell like? How cold were the drops?

How to write your own diary entry about a rainy day

  1. Put the date, day and time at the topAdd Dear Diary on the next line.
  2. Open with the weatherDescribe the sky or the first drops in one or two sentences. Use one sound or color word.
  3. Tell what you did because of the rainStayed in, played outside, got caught on the way home, watched from the window. Keep it in the past tense.
  4. Add your sensesPick at least three: what you saw, heard, smelled and felt.
  5. Say how the rain made you feelHappy, cozy, bored, thoughtful. Give a reason.
  6. Close warmlyEnd with a final thought, a closing like Good night, and your name.

Common mistakes in rainy day diary entries

  • Only saying "it rained". Describe it. How hard? What sound? What smell?
  • Forgetting yourself. It is a diary, not a weather report. Tell what you did and felt.
  • Mixing tenses. Keep the events in the past: it rained, we played, I felt.
  • Missing the date or name. These are part of the format and usually carry marks.
  • Too many big words at once. One strong word like drenched is better than five fancy ones in a row.
  • No ending. Finish with a feeling or a thought, not just the last event.

Questions people ask

How do I describe a rainy day in a diary entry?

Use your senses. Say what the sky looked like, what the rain sounded like on the roof or window, how the wet earth smelled, and how the drops felt on your skin. Then add what you did and how it made you feel. One sound word, one smell and one feeling are enough to bring a short entry to life.

How do you start a diary entry about a rainy day?

Write the date, day and time, then Dear Diary. Your first line can set the scene, for example: Today the sky turned dark gray just after lunch, and soon the rain was drumming on our roof. Starting with the weather straight away tells the reader what the entry is about.

What can I write if nothing happened on the rainy day?

Write about the small things. What did you watch from the window? What did you eat? Who did you talk to? Did you read, draw, sleep or feel bored? A quiet day can make a lovely diary entry if you describe the mood and your thoughts honestly.

How long should a diary entry about a rainy day be?

Follow the word limit in your question. For most class 6 to 10 exams it is about 100 to 150 words. Younger children can write 60 to 100 words. Creative writing homework can be longer, around 200 to 300 words, with more description and feelings.

Is a rainy day diary entry written in past or present tense?

Mainly the past tense, because you are writing about what happened earlier today: it rained, we played, I felt cozy. If it is still raining as you write, you can use the present tense for that one moment, such as The rain is still falling as I write this.
