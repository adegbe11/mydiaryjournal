Journal ideas: 50 things to write and make in your journal

The best journal ideas give you something clear to do when you open the page: a list to make, a memory to record, a question to answer or a small tracker to fill in. Below are 50 ideas grouped by type, each with a line or two on how to do it, so you never stare at a blank page again.

How to use these journaling ideas

You do not need to do all 50. Pick one that matches your mood today. If you have five minutes, choose a list. If you have half an hour and some pens, try a creative page. If something is on your mind, go to the reflection ideas.

Every idea here works in a paper notebook or in our free online journal, which saves on your own device with no sign up.

Write the date at the top of every page, even for lists. When you read back in a year, the date is what turns a list into a memory.

Journal ideas list: quick lists to write

Lists are the easiest way in. There is no need for full sentences, and they are fun to read back later.

  • 1. Three good things. Write three good things that happened today, even small ones like a hot shower or a kind text.
  • 2. Things I want to learn. List skills, languages or hobbies you are curious about. Star the one you could start this month.
  • 3. Songs on repeat. Write the songs you keep playing right now and one line on why each one fits your life at the moment.
  • 4. Small joys. Keep a running list of tiny things that make you happy. Add to it whenever you notice one.
  • 5. Books and shows. Log what you read and watch with a score out of five and a one sentence review.
  • 6. Places I want to go. Mix big trips with local spots, like the cafe across town you have never tried.
  • 7. Things I am proud of. List ten things, from finishing school to fixing a leaky tap. Read it on hard days.
  • 8. Comfort foods. Write the meals that feel like home and who used to make them for you.
  • 9. Questions I have. Write questions you wonder about, big or silly. Answer one later or leave them open.
  • 10. Done list. Instead of a to do list, write what you already finished today. It is a good cure for feeling behind.

Journal ideas to write about your life

These ideas turn your days into a record you can keep. They are great if you want your journal to work like a diary.

  • 11. A day in my life. Write today from waking up to bedtime in simple order. Do this once a year and compare.
  • 12. Letter to future me. Write to yourself one year from now. Say what you hope has changed and what you hope has not.
  • 13. Letter to younger me. Pick an age that was hard and tell that version of you what you know now.
  • 14. Childhood home tour. Walk through the house you grew up in, room by room, and write what you see, smell and hear.
  • 15. People who shaped me. Pick one person per page. Write how you met and one thing they taught you.
  • 16. First times. First job, first concert, first time living alone. Write the details before they fade.
  • 17. This week in one page. Every Sunday, sum up the week in five lines: best moment, hardest moment, one lesson, one photo you would take, one thing for next week.
  • 18. Overheard and remembered. Write funny or wise things people say, with who said it and when.
  • 19. My current favorites. Snack, outfit, app, word, smell, person to text. Repeat each season.
  • 20. The story behind an object. Pick something on your desk or shelf and write where it came from.

Trackers and logs to keep in a journal

Trackers take seconds to fill in and show you patterns over weeks. Draw a simple grid with the days of the month across the top.

  • 21. Mood tracker. Color one square a day for how you felt. Pick five colors and write a key. See our mood journal guide for layouts.
  • 22. Sleep log. Write what time you went to bed and woke up. After a month, look at which nights felt best.
  • 23. Habit tracker. List three to five habits down the side and tick each day you do them. Keep it short so it stays doable.
  • 24. Water and steps. A row of eight small circles for glasses of water, plus your step count.
  • 25. Spending log. Write every purchase for one week. You do not have to change anything, just notice.
  • 26. Reading log. Pages or minutes read each day, plus the title. Good for kids and adults.
  • 27. Workout log. Date, what you did, how long, and how you felt after.
  • 28. Food and feelings. Write what you ate and how you felt an hour later. A simple food log can show you surprising patterns.
  • 29. Gratitude streak. One thing you are thankful for each day, written in a tiny box per date.
  • 30. Weather and wins. A tiny weather doodle next to one win for the day. It makes a cute page that is easy to keep.

Creative journal ideas

These are for days when you want to play more than think. You do not need to be good at art.

  • 31. Collage page. Glue in tickets, receipts, wrappers and photos from one week. Write one line next to each.
  • 32. Doodle your day. Draw five tiny pictures that sum up the day, like a coffee cup, a bus and a moon.
  • 33. Color palette page. Pick three colors that match your mood and fill a page with stripes or blobs of them.
  • 34. Six word story. Sum up your day, your year or your life in exactly six words.
  • 35. Map of my neighborhood. Draw your street and mark the places that matter to you.
  • 36. Pressed flowers. Press a flower or leaf from a walk and tape it in with the date and place.
  • 37. Write a poem. Try a short form like a haiku: five syllables, seven, then five. Write about something you saw today.
  • 38. Dream page. Write last night's dream as soon as you wake up, then draw one scene from it.
  • 39. Quote page. Copy out a line that stuck with you from a book or a friend, then write why. See journal ideas with quotes.
  • 40. Bucket list page. Write 25 things you want to do in your life. Decorate the border and tick them off over the years.

Journal ideas for reflection and growth

Use these when something is on your mind or when you want to understand yourself better.

  • 41. Brain dump. Set a timer for ten minutes and write everything in your head without stopping. Circle anything that needs action.
  • 42. Worry and plan. Write a worry on the left side of the page. On the right, write one small step you can take about it.
  • 43. What went well, what did not. End each week with two short columns. Be honest and kind on both sides.
  • 44. My values. List five things that matter most to you, like family or honesty. Write one way you lived each one this week.
  • 45. Year in review. In December, answer: best day, hardest day, biggest change, what I want more of next year.
  • 46. Goals for the month. Three goals at most. Break each one into the first tiny step.
  • 47. Hard conversation practice. Write what you want to say to someone before you say it. It often makes the real talk calmer.
  • 48. Forgiveness page. Write about something you are still holding on to and what letting go might look like.
  • 49. Morning pages. Three pages of free writing first thing in the morning, before you check your phone.
  • 50. Answer one prompt. Pick a question from our list of journal prompts and write for five minutes.

Tips to keep your journal going

  1. Keep it smallOne idea per day is plenty. Five minutes you actually do beats an hour you keep putting off.
  2. Tie it to a habitJournal with your morning coffee or right after you brush your teeth at night.
  3. Keep an idea bankCopy ten ideas from this page onto the inside cover. When you feel stuck, pick one.
  4. Let it be messyCrossed out words and crooked lines are fine. Nobody grades a journal.
  5. Read back once a monthLooking back is the reward. You will see how much you have done and how you have grown.

Questions people ask

What should I put in my journal?

Anything that helps you remember, think or create. Good starting points are a list of three good things, a short summary of your day, a mood tracker, a letter to your future self, or a collage of tickets and photos. If you need more help, try our guide on what to write in a journal.

How do I start a journal when I have no ideas?

Start with a list, because lists do not need full sentences. Write the date, then three things you did today and one thing you are looking forward to. Once the page is not blank, writing more feels much easier. Our how to start a journal guide walks you through the first week.

What are the different types of journaling?

Common types include daily journals, gratitude journals, bullet journals, dream journals, travel journals, art journals, junk journals, prayer journals and reflective journals. Many people mix types in one notebook, for example a daily entry plus a small tracker and a doodle.

How often should I journal?

There is no rule. Many people write a few lines daily because it builds a habit. Others journal a few times a week or only when something big happens. Pick a rhythm you can keep, and do not worry about missed days.

What can I do with an empty journal?

Turn it into something with one purpose: a gratitude journal, a reading log, a travel journal, a recipe book, a dream diary or a five year memory book. Giving the notebook a job makes it much easier to start filling it.
