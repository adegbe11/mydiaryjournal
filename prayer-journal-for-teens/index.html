Prayer journal for teens: 78 honest prompts

A prayer journal for teens is a private place to talk to God about real life: friends, school, family, your future, and the stuff you do not say out loud. Below are 78 prompts sorted by area of life, plus a page where you can write right now. Everything you type stays on this device.

How to use a prayer journal as a teen

Prayer does not need fancy words. God wants the real you, including the doubts, the bad days and the questions. Writing helps you slow down and actually say what you mean.

  • Pick one prompt. Tap Write and it lands on your page above.
  • Write like you text a close friend. Short, honest, no filters.
  • Add a verse when you can. Read a few verses first, even just one psalm.
  • Keep track of answers. When something changes, go back and write what happened.
  • It is private. Your page saves on this device only. No account, no one else sees it.

Prompts about identity and who you are

  1. What do you think God sees when He looks at you? Now write what you see.
  2. Where do you feel pressure to be someone you are not?
  3. What is one thing about yourself you wish you could change? Talk to God about it.
  4. Read Psalm 139:13-16. Write a prayer about being made on purpose.
  5. What does your social media say about you? Is it the real you?
  6. Where do you feel most like the person God made you to be?
  7. What gifts or talents has God given you? Thank Him for them.
  8. Who do you compare yourself to the most? Ask God to help you let it go.
  9. Write about a time you felt really seen or understood.
  10. What would change if you believed God was proud of you?
  11. Ask God to show you one true thing about yourself this week.
  12. What is a lie you believe about yourself? Write what God says instead.

Prompts about friends and relationships

  1. Who are your closest friends right now? Pray for each of them by name.
  2. Is there a friendship that is hurting you? Ask God what to do.
  3. Do you feel left out of anything right now? Tell God how it feels.
  4. Who at school seems lonely? Ask God how you could be kind to them.
  5. What kind of friend do you want to be?
  6. Is there drama you are caught in? Ask for wisdom and a calm heart.
  7. If you have a crush or are dating, talk to God honestly about it.
  8. What do you want in a future relationship? Ask God to guide you.
  9. Pray for a friend who does not know Jesus.
  10. Is there someone you need to forgive? Write about it.
  11. Thank God for a friend who has been there for you.

Prompts about family

  1. What is going on at home right now? Tell God everything.
  2. Pray for your mom, dad or guardian by name.
  3. Is there tension with a parent? Ask God to help you understand each other.
  4. Pray for your brothers or sisters. What are they going through?
  5. What is something you appreciate about your family that you never say?
  6. Ask God to help you respect your parents even when you disagree.
  7. If your home is hard, tell God what you need most.
  8. Write a prayer for your grandparents or extended family.
  9. What is one way you could make home a little better this week?
  10. Thank God for a good memory with your family.

If home does not feel safe, or you are being hurt, please tell a trusted adult like a teacher, school counselor or pastor. If you are in danger or crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, you can call or text 988 any time.

Prompts about school and the future

  1. What test, project or class is stressing you out? Give it to God.
  2. Ask God for focus and a good attitude at school this week.
  3. What do you hope to do after high school? Talk to God about it.
  4. Are you afraid of failing at something? Write about it honestly.
  5. Pray for your teachers and coaches by name.
  6. What decision is coming up that you need wisdom for?
  7. Ask God what He might want you to do with your life.
  8. Write about a time you worked hard and God helped you.
  9. Is there a sport, club or job you want? Ask God about it.
  10. Ask God to help you do your best without making grades your identity.
  11. What would you try if you were not scared of what people think?

Prompts for anxiety, stress and hard days

  1. What are the top three things you are worried about? Give each one to God.
  2. What thoughts keep you up at night? Write them down and pray over them.
  3. Where do you feel the most pressure right now?
  4. Tell God what a bad day feels like for you.
  5. What helps you calm down? Thank God for it.
  6. Read Matthew 11:28. What do you need rest from?
  7. Is your phone making you feel worse? Ask God for help with it.
  8. Write about a hard time God already brought you through.
  9. What would you say to a friend feeling what you feel? Say it to yourself.
  10. Ask God to give you peace before you fall asleep tonight.
  11. Is there anyone you can talk to about this? Pray for the courage to reach out.

Journaling can help, but it is not a replacement for talking with a counselor or doctor. If you feel hopeless or think about hurting yourself, tell an adult now. In the US, call or text 988. See also Christian journal prompts for anxiety.

Prompts about faith and doubts

  1. What is a question you have about God that you have never asked?
  2. Do you believe because of your parents, or because you have met God yourself?
  3. Write about a time God felt real to you.
  4. When does God feel far away? Tell Him honestly.
  5. What is a Bible story you want to understand better?
  6. What does following Jesus look like at your school?
  7. Is there a sin you keep struggling with? Talk to God about it and ask for help.
  8. Thank Jesus for one thing He did for you.
  9. What does church feel like for you right now? Be honest.
  10. Ask God to make your faith your own.
  11. Pray for your youth group or youth leader.

Prayer journal prompts for teen girls

These prompts were written with teen girls in mind, but anyone can use them.

  1. What do you see when you look in the mirror? Ask God to help you see what He sees.
  2. Where do you feel pressure about how you look? Write about it.
  3. Who are the women you look up to? Thank God for them.
  4. Write a prayer for your future self at 25.
  5. Is there a girl you are jealous of? Pray for her and for your own heart.
  6. Ask God to help you set healthy boundaries with friends and guys.
  7. What makes you feel strong? Thank God for it.
  8. Write about a woman in the Bible you relate to.
  9. Ask God to help you speak kindly about yourself and others.
  10. Pray for the girls in your class, the popular ones and the quiet ones.
  11. What dream do you want to bring to God?
  12. Write a prayer of thanks for being exactly who He made you.

Tips for keeping your prayer journal going

  • Same time, same place. Right before bed or right after school works for a lot of teens.
  • Set a small goal. Three sentences a day is a win.
  • Do not wait to feel spiritual. Write anyway. Feelings often follow.
  • Read old pages once a month. You will be surprised how much has changed.
  • Try different formats. A prayer journal template with boxes, or a free letter to God. See more prayer journal ideas.

Questions people ask

How do teens start a prayer journal?

Grab a notebook or use the page above. Write the date, read a short Bible passage, and then write to God like you would text a close friend. Pick one prompt if you are stuck. Keep it short and try to write at the same time each day, like before bed.

What should a teenager write in a prayer journal?

Anything real. Thanks for good things, stress about school, problems with friends or family, questions about faith, things you are sorry for, and prayers for other people. God wants your honest words, not perfect ones.

Is it okay to write my doubts in a prayer journal?

Yes. Many of the psalms are full of honest questions and complaints to God. Writing doubts down is a way to bring them to God instead of hiding them. It can also help to talk them over with a youth leader or pastor you trust.

Will anyone see what I write on this page?

No. Your writing is saved in the browser on this device only. It is not sent to us and there is no account. If others use the same device, they could open the page, so you may want to clear it or print it when you are done.

This page has a section of prompts written for teen girls, covering body image, friendships, jealousy, boundaries and dreams. Any notebook can become a prayer journal for teen girls, and you can use these prompts with it.
