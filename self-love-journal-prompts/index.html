What self love journaling looks like

Self love is not about thinking you are perfect. It is about being on your own side. It means speaking to yourself fairly, taking care of your needs and letting yourself be a work in progress.

Journaling helps because many of us never notice how harshly we talk to ourselves until we see it written down. Once you see it, you can choose a kinder sentence.

  • Start where you are. If writing something nice about yourself feels fake, write something neutral and true instead, like I showed up today.
  • Write to yourself in the second person. Using you instead of I can make kindness feel easier, like writing to a friend.
  • Keep a running list. Add one thing you like about yourself each day and read the list on hard days.

Journal prompts for self love and kind self-talk

  1. What is something harsh I said to myself this week? Rewrite it as a caring friend would say it.
  2. Write a letter to myself from the point of view of someone who adores me.
  3. What are five things I like about my personality?
  4. What do I wish someone had told me when I was younger? Tell myself now.
  5. When do I feel most at home in my own skin?
  6. What nickname or phrase could I use to talk to myself gently?
  7. What would change if I trusted my own judgment a little more?
  8. Which part of me do I find hardest to love? What might it be trying to do for me?
  9. What did I do today that a kinder version of me would praise?
  10. What does being my own best friend look like on an ordinary day?
  11. Write a short pep talk I can read before a hard conversation.
  12. Which of my quirks would I miss if they disappeared?

Self love journal prompts about your body

Body image is a tender topic. Go slowly and focus on what your body does, not only how it looks.

  1. List ten things my body let me do this week, from hugging to climbing stairs.
  2. Which part of my body am I grateful for today, and why?
  3. How do I talk about my body when I am with friends? Would I talk about theirs that way?
  4. What kind of movement feels like a gift instead of a punishment?
  5. What clothes make me feel comfortable and like myself?
  6. Which social media accounts make me feel worse about my body? Could I mute them?
  7. What would I do this summer if I stopped waiting for my body to change first?
  8. Describe a physical feeling I love: warm sun, a soft blanket, cold water.
  9. What has my body carried me through that I rarely give it credit for?
  10. Write a thank you note to my hands, feet or heart.

Prompts about boundaries and saying no

Loving yourself includes protecting your time and energy. For more on people, see journal prompts on relationships.

  1. Where in my life do I say yes when I mean no?
  2. What is one boundary I need but have been afraid to set?
  3. How do I feel after spending time with people who respect my limits?
  4. What would I say if I could turn down a request without explaining?
  5. Which relationship takes more than it gives right now?
  6. What does my energy feel like after a day with no plans?
  7. What guilt shows up when I put myself first? Is it fair?
  8. Who in my life models healthy boundaries? What can I learn from them?
  9. What is one thing I will stop doing just to keep the peace?
  10. Write a polite but firm sentence I can use to say no this week.

Self love journal prompts for forgiveness and mistakes

  1. What is a mistake I still punish myself for? What did I learn from it?
  2. If my best friend had made that same mistake, would I forgive them?
  3. Write a letter of forgiveness to my younger self.
  4. What did I do the best I could with what I knew at the time?
  5. What would it feel like to put down the guilt I have been carrying?
  6. How have my mistakes made me more understanding of others?
  7. What is one thing I need to apologize to myself for?
  8. Which old regret is ready to be filed away, not erased?
  9. What does a fresh start look like for me this week?
  10. How can I treat my next mistake with more patience?

Self love journal prompts for women

Many women carry extra pressure to look a certain way, keep everyone happy and do it all without help. These prompts speak to that. Anyone can use them. See more in journal prompts for women.

  1. Where am I trying to be everything for everyone? What would happen if I did a little less?
  2. Which women in my life make me feel strong? What do they see in me?
  3. What rule about being a good woman did I grow up with that I no longer believe?
  4. What do I want just for me, not for my family, partner or job?
  5. How do I feel when I take up space, speak first or ask for more?
  6. What would I do differently if I stopped worrying about being liked?
  7. Which part of being a woman do I love most?
  8. When did I last do something only because it made me happy?
  9. What would I tell a younger woman starting out in my shoes?
  10. What does my version of beauty look like when nobody else is judging?

Prompts to celebrate yourself

  1. What is an accomplishment I never properly celebrated? Celebrate it here.
  2. List twenty small things I have done well this year.
  3. What makes me a good friend, partner, sibling or coworker?
  4. Which of my values am I proud to live by?
  5. What hard season did I survive that I barely talk about?
  6. What talent do I have that I keep downplaying?
  7. What is something I do that makes other people's lives better?
  8. Describe a time I surprised myself with how strong I was.
  9. What do I love about the way my mind works?
  10. If I threw myself a party, what would the speech say?
  11. What kind compliment from someone else can I finally accept as true?
  12. What do I want to keep doing just because it is very me?
  13. Write a list of reasons I deserve rest, fun and good things.
  14. What does loving myself look like tomorrow, in one small action?
  15. Write a short love letter to myself and sign it.
  16. What would I want to read on the hardest day of my year?
  17. Describe myself the way a grandparent who adores me would.
  18. Which small promise to myself did I keep this month?
  19. What is one thing about me that has always been enough?
  20. How will I remind myself of these answers next week?
  21. What does it feel like to say I like who I am, out loud or on paper?
  22. What would my life look like a year from now if I treated myself this kindly every day?
  23. Who am I when I am not trying to impress anyone?

Tips for keeping a self love journal

  • Fake it gently. If kind words feel awkward, write them anyway. Over time they start to feel true.
  • Notice your inner critic. When you write something harsh, underline it and answer it in a kinder voice.
  • Mix in care. Pair a self love prompt with one act from our self-care journal prompts.
  • Add affirmations. Write one sentence to repeat during the day. Our affirmation journal prompts show you how.
  • Keep it for you. This diary saves in your browser only, so you can be fully honest.

If you struggle with deep feelings of worthlessness or thoughts of harming yourself, journaling is not enough on its own. Please reach out to a doctor or counselor. In the US, you can call or text 988 at any time.

Questions people ask

What is a self love journal?

A self love journal is a place to practice treating yourself kindly. You write answers to prompts about what you like about yourself, how you talk to yourself, your body, your boundaries and your wins. Over time, it helps you notice harsh self-talk and replace it with something fairer and warmer.

How do I start a self love journal?

Start small. Each day, write one thing you did well and one kind sentence to yourself. A few times a week, answer one of the prompts above. If kind words feel strange, begin with neutral facts like I got through a busy day. The habit matters more than the length.

What should I write in a self love journal?

Good things to write include what you like about yourself, things your body did for you, mistakes you are ready to forgive, boundaries you want to set and wins you never celebrated. You can also write letters to your younger self or affirmations to read on hard days.

Is self love selfish?

No. Taking care of yourself and speaking to yourself kindly gives you more energy and patience for others. Self love is not about putting yourself above everyone. It is about not leaving yourself off the list. Setting healthy limits often makes relationships better, not worse.

What if writing nice things about myself feels fake?

That is very common. Start with things that are simply true, like I showed up for work or I made dinner. Then try writing to yourself as you would to a friend. Kindness can feel fake at first and still be worth practicing. It usually gets easier with time.
