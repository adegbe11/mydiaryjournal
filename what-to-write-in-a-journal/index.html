How to journal

What to write in a journal: ideas, examples and a simple formula

You can write anything in a journal: what happened today, how you feel, what you are grateful for, what worries you, what you want, and what you learned. If you are stuck, use a simple formula: one thing that happened, one feeling, one thought about it. Three sentences is a real journal entry.

The easy formula for any journal entry

Most people freeze because they think a journal entry has to be deep or well written. It does not. Nobody grades it. A good entry is simply an honest one.

When you do not know what to write in a journal entry, answer these three questions in order:

  1. What happened? One moment from today, big or small.
  2. How did I feel? Name the feeling in one or two words.
  3. What do I think about it? Why it mattered, what you learned, or what you want to do next.

If even three sentences feels like too much, write one line. A one line a day journal is still a journal.

What to write in a journal every day

If you want to write daily, it helps to have a few go-to topics so you never start from nothing. Rotate through these:

  • Your day in three lines. Morning, afternoon, evening.
  • Three good things. Small is fine: a hot shower, a kind text, a song.
  • Your mood and why. Rate it from 1 to 10, then explain the number.
  • One thing you learned. A fact, a skill, or something about a person.
  • Tomorrow's one goal. The single thing that would make tomorrow feel good.
  • A conversation you keep thinking about. What was said and what you wish you had said.
  • What your body needs. Sleep, water, a walk, a break.
  • A question for yourself. Write it down, even if you cannot answer it yet.

Write the hard days and the good days

It is tempting to only write when something goes wrong, or only when life looks nice. Try to record both. Years later, the entries about hard times often mean the most, because they show you how you got through. The good entries remind you that bad stretches end.

Notice small details

Instead of retelling your whole day, pick one moment that stirred a feeling and describe it closely: who was there, what you heard, what the light looked like, what you said. Small details are what bring a memory back, and noticing them is a skill that grows the more you write.

Different ways to write it down

A journal entry does not have to be paragraphs. If writing full sentences feels like a chore, change the shape:

  • Lists. Five things that annoyed you, five songs on repeat, five things to do this weekend.
  • Bullet notes. Short fragments, like "slept badly, rainy, good chat with Leo."
  • Dialogue. Write a conversation as a script, including what you wish you had said.
  • A letter. To a friend, a family member, your younger self, or God. You never have to send it.
  • A short poem. It does not need to rhyme. Three lines about the day is enough.
  • Doodles and sketches. Draw your mood as weather, or sketch the view from where you sit.
  • A to do list for tomorrow. Ending the day by writing tomorrow's tasks helps many people sleep.

What not to worry about

  • Spelling and grammar. Your journal is not homework. Write the way you talk.
  • Being interesting. Ordinary days are worth recording. They are the ones you forget first.
  • Writing every day. Skipping is normal. Just come back.
  • Being positive. Hard feelings belong in a journal too. That is often where it helps most.
  • Someone reading it. Keep it somewhere private. Our private diary with lock has a PIN for shared computers.

Tips when you are stuck

  1. Start with the date and the weatherIt sounds silly, but writing anything gets your hand moving and the rest often follows.
  2. Write "Right now I feel"Finish the sentence. Then write "because" and keep going.
  3. Make a listLists feel easier than paragraphs. Five things that annoyed you, five things you want, five people you love.
  4. Set a five minute timerWrite without stopping until it rings. If you have nothing to say, write "I have nothing to say" until something comes.
  5. Use a promptPick one from this page or from journaling prompts for beginners and answer it in a few lines.

Questions people ask

What do you write in a journal for beginners?

Start with your day in three sentences: what happened, how you felt, and what you think about it. You can also list three things you are grateful for or answer one simple prompt. Keep entries short at first so the habit is easy to keep. Length can grow later once writing feels natural.

What should I write in my journal every day?

Try a short routine: the date, your mood from 1 to 10, one thing that happened, one thing you are grateful for, and one goal for tomorrow. That takes about five minutes. On days you have more to say, keep writing. On busy days, one line is enough.

Is it okay to write negative things in a journal?

Yes. A journal is a safe place for anger, sadness and worry. Writing hard feelings down can make them feel smaller and clearer. If you notice you only write when upset, add one good thing at the end of each entry so you see the full picture of your life.

How long should a journal entry be?

There is no right length. One line counts. Many people write 100 to 300 words, which takes five to ten minutes. Short entries you actually write beat long entries you keep putting off.

What do you write on the first page of a journal?

Many people write their name, the date they started, and a short note about why they are starting. You could also write a letter to your future self or list what is happening in your life right now. Our guide on how to start a journal has first page ideas.

What do you write in a journal when nothing happened?

Write what you did anyway, even if it was the same as yesterday. You can also describe one small detail you noticed, how you feel right now, a list of what is on your mind, or answer one prompt. Quiet days are worth recording. They show what normal life looked like, which is often what people most want to remember later.
