Journal examples for students

A student journal is a short piece of writing where you describe something you learned or did, then reflect on what it meant and what you will do next. Teachers usually want three things: what happened, what you think about it, and what you learned. Below are journal examples for students at school and college, plus the two reflection models most classes use.

What teachers look for in a student journal

A class journal is not just a summary. Teachers want to see your thinking, not just repeat what was said in class. The best entries show that you understood something, questioned something, or changed your mind. A strong student journal entry:

  • Describes briefly. One or two sentences on what happened. Do not retell the whole lesson.
  • Reflects honestly. How you felt, what surprised you, what confused you.
  • Connects. Links the experience to class ideas, readings or your own life.
  • Looks forward. Says what you will do differently or want to learn next.
  • Uses "I". Reflective journals are personal. First person is expected.

Always check your teacher's instructions first. Some want a set length, a set model, or references to readings.

Model 1: What? So what? Now what?

This is the simplest reflection model and a favorite in schools. It has three questions:

  1. What? What happened? Describe the event or lesson in plain words.
  2. So what? Why does it matter? What did you feel, notice or learn?
  3. Now what? What will you do next because of it?

This model works for almost any subject: a science lab, a book chapter, a field trip, or a group project.

Model 2: Gibbs reflective cycle, in plain words

The Gibbs reflective cycle was created by Graham Gibbs in 1988. It is common in college courses like nursing, teaching and social work. It has six stages you go through in order:

  1. DescriptionWhat happened? Just the facts.
  2. FeelingsWhat were you thinking and feeling at the time?
  3. EvaluationWhat was good and what was bad about the experience?
  4. AnalysisWhy did things go the way they did? Link to what you have learned in class.
  5. ConclusionWhat else could you have done? What did you learn?
  6. Action planIf it happened again, what would you do?

Worked example: Gibbs cycle

For a graded Gibbs reflection, the analysis stage usually earns the most marks. Link what happened to a reading, theory or lecture.

Journal types students keep (with examples)

Not every student journal is a graded reflection. Here are other kinds teachers use, and that students keep on their own.

  • Daily highlights journal: the best moment of the school day.
  • Gratitude journal: good things, people and small wins.
  • Project journal: notes, progress and lessons for one big assignment, hobby or sport.
  • School journal: questions, homework and what you learned each week. It doubles as a planner.
  • Dream journal: dreams written down right after waking.
  • Creative journal: writing mixed with drawing, collage or poems.
  • Feelings journal: a private place for worries, friendships and stress.

Weak vs strong: the same entry two ways

The easiest way to improve a student journal is to compare a weak entry with a strong one about the same event.

The weak entry only lists events. The strong entry picks one moment, explains why it mattered, links it to class, and ends with a question. That is what teachers mean by reflection.

Reflective journal sample for students: phrases that help

Stuck on how to word it? These sentence starters fit most student journals:

  • At first I thought... but now I think...
  • This surprised me because...
  • I felt... when...
  • This connects to what we learned about...
  • One thing I would do differently is...
  • I still have questions about...
  • Next time I will...

Tips for a better student journal

  • Write soon after. Reflect the same day while details are fresh.
  • Be honest about mistakes. Teachers reward real reflection more than perfect stories.
  • Keep description short. Spend most of your words on "so what" and "now what."
  • Use one example. Go deep on one moment instead of listing everything.
  • Reread before you submit. Check you answered every part of the assignment.

Questions people ask

How do you write a journal entry for school?

Start with the date and topic. Briefly describe what happened or what you learned. Then explain what you think about it, how it made you feel, and how it connects to class. End with what you will do next. The What? So what? Now what? model is an easy way to cover all three parts.

What is a reflective journal for students?

It is a journal where students write about their learning experiences and think about what they mean. Instead of just summarizing a lesson, you explain what you understood, what confused you, how you felt, and how you will use it. Many courses grade these entries.

How long should a student journal entry be?

It depends on the assignment. School journals are often 100 to 250 words. College reflective journals are often 300 to 800 words. If your teacher does not say, aim for enough to describe briefly and reflect in depth, and check the rubric.

What is the Gibbs reflective cycle?

It is a six-stage model for reflecting on an experience, created by Graham Gibbs in 1988. The stages are description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion and action plan. It is widely used in nursing, teaching and other college courses to structure reflective writing.

Can I use "I" in a reflective journal?

Yes. Reflective journals are about your own thoughts and experiences, so writing in the first person is expected. Keep it clear and thoughtful, and support your points with examples or links to class material when asked.

What are some journal ideas for college students?

College students often keep reflective journals for placements, learning logs for hard courses, reading responses, and personal journals for stress and goals. Weekly prompts like "What idea changed how I think this week?" or "What am I struggling with and who could help?" work well. Keep entries honest and link them to what you are learning.
