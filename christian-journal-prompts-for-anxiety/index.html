How to use these anxiety journal prompts as a Christian

Journaling and prayer can help with worry, but they are not a replacement for professional care. If anxiety is making daily life hard, please talk to a doctor or a licensed counselor. Many Christians see both a counselor and a pastor. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, contact local emergency services now. In the US, call or text 988.

Anxiety often feels like a crowd of thoughts all talking at once. Writing slows them down so you can look at them one by one and hand them to God. Research on expressive writing has found it can help some people process stress, and the Bible invites us again and again to bring our cares to the Lord.

  1. Breathe firstTake three slow breaths. You can pray one short line with each, like: Lord, You are here.
  2. Pick one promptChoose the one that fits right now. You do not need to answer more than one.
  3. Write without fixingLet it be messy. Spelling and neat sentences do not matter.
  4. End with one truthClose with a verse reference or one line about who God is, and a short prayer.

Prompts to name your worries

  1. What is the thought that keeps coming back today? Write it exactly as it sounds in my head.
  2. Where do I feel this worry in my body right now: chest, stomach, shoulders, jaw?
  3. On a scale of 1 to 10, how anxious am I? What would make it one number lower?
  4. What started this feeling today? Was there a moment, a message or a memory?
  5. What am I afraid will happen? Write the whole fear, start to finish.
  6. Is this worry about today, next week, or something years away?
  7. Which part of this is in my control, and which part is not?
  8. What have I been avoiding because it makes me anxious?
  9. What would I say if I told God about this worry like I would tell a close friend?
  10. When was the last time I felt calm? What was different?
  11. What is the story I keep telling myself about this situation?
  12. If this worry were a weight, how heavy would it be, and what would it be like to set it down?

Prompts to bring your anxiety to God in prayer

  1. Write a prayer that starts: Lord, this is what is keeping me awake.
  2. List each worry on its own line. After each one, write: I give this to You.
  3. What do I want God to do about this? Ask Him plainly.
  4. Write a prayer for the people involved in what I am worried about.
  5. What would it look like to trust God with this for just today, not forever?
  6. Write a breath prayer, a short line I can pray in and out, for this week.
  7. Which psalm sounds like how I feel? Write my own version of it.
  8. What do I need to confess that is feeding my fear, like control, pride or hiding?
  9. Write a prayer of honest lament: what hurts, what I need, and that I still choose to trust Him.
  10. What would I pray if I knew God was smiling at me while I prayed?
  11. Who can I ask to pray with me about this? Write their name and what I will tell them.

Prompts to remember what is true

  1. Write my fear in one column and what I know about God in the other.
  2. Which verse has helped me before when I was afraid? Write the reference and what it reminds me of.
  3. What does it mean that God is with me in this exact room, right now?
  4. What has God already carried me through that once felt just as big?
  5. If Jesus sat beside me while I worried, what do I think His face would look like?
  6. Which of my anxious thoughts is a guess, and which is a fact?
  7. What would I tell a friend who had this same worry?
  8. What does the Bible say God does with my tomorrow?
  9. Write five true things about God, one per line.
  10. What would peace look like in this situation, even if nothing changed yet?
  11. How has God been faithful to me in the past month?

Prompts for anxious nights and hard mornings

  1. Before bed: what can I put down tonight and pick up again tomorrow, if I still need to?
  2. What went better today than I feared it would?
  3. Write one thing to do first tomorrow, so my mind can stop planning.
  4. What would I like to hand to God before I sleep?
  5. Write a short prayer for rest, for my body and my mind.
  6. Morning: how did I sleep, and how is my heart as the day begins?
  7. What is one thing today I can do slowly and on purpose?
  8. Which part of today worries me most? Write a prayer just for that hour.
  9. Who will I see today that I can be kind to?
  10. What small thing would make today feel safe and steady?
  11. Write a verse reference on my hand or a sticky note. Which one did I choose and why?

Prompts for gratitude and peace

  1. List three things God gave me today, even tiny ones.
  2. Describe one place where I feel peaceful. What makes it so?
  3. What sounds, smells and sights around me right now can I thank God for?
  4. Who has been gentle with me lately, and how can I thank them?
  5. What is one thing I worried about last year that turned out fine?
  6. Write a thank you prayer for my body, which works hard even when I am anxious.
  7. What simple pleasure helps me feel calm: a walk, a song, a cup of tea?
  8. What answered prayer could I read again today?
  9. Where did I see beauty this week that I almost missed?
  10. What would a slow, peaceful Sunday look like for me?
  11. Write a short psalm of thanks in my own words.

Prompts for small steps and support

  1. What is one small, kind thing I can do for myself today?
  2. Which habit makes my anxiety worse, like scrolling late at night? What could I try instead?
  3. Who could I talk to this week: a friend, a pastor, a counselor or a doctor?
  4. What would I need to feel ready to ask for help?
  5. What is one boundary that would protect my peace?
  6. What can I take off my schedule this week?
  7. How can I care for my body today: water, food, sleep, a short walk?
  8. What is the smallest first step on the thing I have been avoiding?
  9. What would I like to remember the next time anxiety rises?
  10. Write a short note to my future anxious self from my calm self today.
  11. Where can I serve someone else this week in a small way?
  12. Which song or hymn calms me? Write the title and why it helps.
  13. What would I like to tell a counselor or pastor if I had ten minutes with them?
  14. Write a closing prayer for this season, asking God for courage and rest.

Tips for journaling with anxiety

  • Keep it short. Five minutes is enough. Long sessions can make some people spiral.
  • End on truth. Always finish with a verse reference, a thank you, or a short prayer.
  • Do not reread anxious pages late at night. Read them later, in daylight, to see how God answered.
  • Watch for patterns. If the same worry shows up every week, it may be worth talking through with someone you trust.
  • Be gentle with yourself. Feeling anxious is not a sign of weak faith. Many faithful people in the Bible felt afraid.
  • Get support. A counselor, doctor or pastor can help in ways a journal cannot.

If you ever feel unsafe or think about harming yourself, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline any time, day or night.

Questions people ask

How do Christians journal for anxiety?

Start with a short prayer or a few slow breaths. Write the worry down plainly, then write what is in your control and what is not. Bring it to God in a written prayer and end with a verse reference or a true statement about Him. Keep it short and finish on hope, not on the worry.

What Bible verses help with anxiety?

Many Christians turn to Philippians 4:6-7, 1 Peter 5:7, Isaiah 41:10, Matthew 6:34, Matthew 11:28 and Psalm 94:19. Write one reference at the top of your journal page and read it slowly before and after you write.

Is anxiety a sin or a lack of faith?

Feeling anxious is not the same as sinning. Many faithful people in the Bible felt fear and distress and brought it to God. The Bible invites us to pray about our worries. Anxiety can also have physical causes, so it is wise to talk with a doctor or counselor as well as a pastor.

Can journaling replace counseling for anxiety?

No. Journaling can help you notice and pray through your worries, but it is not a replacement for a doctor or licensed counselor. If anxiety is affecting your sleep, work or relationships, please reach out for help. If you are in crisis, in the US call or text 988.

How often should I journal when I feel anxious?

Short and regular works best. Try five minutes once a day, or whenever a worry keeps circling. Some people use a quick evening entry to set their worries down before bed. If writing makes you feel worse, stop and try a walk, a prayer or talking with someone.
