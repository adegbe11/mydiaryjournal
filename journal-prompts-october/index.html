October journal prompts: a prompt for every day

October journal prompts help you enjoy peak fall: bright leaves, cool nights, pumpkins and Halloween. Here is a prompt for each of October's 31 days, plus extra sets for Halloween, cozy autumn evenings and facing your fears. Tap Write on any prompt to answer it in the diary above.

How to use these October journal prompts

October is a great month for journaling. The evenings get longer, the weather pulls you indoors, and Halloween brings out stories and memories. Answer today's prompt, or save the spooky story prompts for the last week of the month. The facing fears set is gentler than it sounds. It is about courage, not horror.

31 October journal prompts: one for each day

Each number matches the date in October, ending with Halloween on the 31st.

Days 1 to 7

  1. October is here. What is your favorite thing about this month, and why?
  2. Describe the best tree you have seen this fall, color by color.
  3. Write about a fall tradition you have kept since childhood.
  4. Write about the first fire, fire pit or candle you lit this fall.
  5. Write about carving or decorating a pumpkin. What face did you give it?
  6. What does a good autumn walk look like for you?
  7. Write about a place that feels a little haunted to you: a house, a road or a building.

Days 8 to 14

  1. What were you worried about this time last year? How did it turn out?
  2. Describe the sound of leaves under your feet, or the wind in the trees.
  3. What helps your mood as the days get shorter?
  4. Write about a time you were brave even though you were scared.
  5. What memory still gives you goosebumps, good or bad?
  6. Who would you most like to share a cup of cider with, and what would you talk about?
  7. What is one thing you have been avoiding? What is the worst that could happen if you faced it?

Days 15 to 21

  1. You are halfway through October. What has been the coziest moment so far?
  2. Write about a full moon night you remember.
  3. Write about a football game, homecoming or school dance you remember from your teen years.
  4. What is your favorite scary movie or book, and why does it work on you?
  5. Describe a rainy October afternoon spent indoors.
  6. What is one thing you would like to let go of before winter comes?
  7. Find an old photo of yourself taken in the fall. What was happening in your life then?

Days 22 to 28

  1. What is a story your family tells every year?
  2. What makes you feel safe on a dark night?
  3. Write about your favorite Halloween costume ever, and who helped you make it.
  4. Which candy did you trade away as a kid, and which did you hide for yourself?
  5. Write about a time you felt like an outsider. What helped?
  6. What do you know now about fear that you did not know as a child?
  7. Describe the smell of your home on a cool autumn evening.

Days 29 to 31

  1. Which fall moment this year would you put in a time capsule?
  2. Write about a neighbor or neighborhood that made trick or treating special.
  3. It is Halloween. Write about tonight: the costumes, the doorbell, the dark.

Halloween journal prompts

Fun for adults and teens. Kids can find gentler ideas in our journal prompts for kids.

  1. If you could be any character for one night, who would you be and what would you do?
  2. Write a short ghost story set in your own home.
  3. What was the scariest house on your street growing up?
  4. Describe a haunted house or corn maze you walked through. Did you scream?
  5. Write a story that starts: The last trick or treater knocked at exactly midnight.
  6. What is a spooky legend from your town or family?
  7. If your house had a friendly ghost, what would its name be and what would it do all day?
  8. What do you love more about Halloween now that you are older, and what do you miss?
  9. Write about the best party or costume contest you have been to.
  10. Invent a new Halloween tradition for your family or friends.

Cozy autumn journal ideas for October evenings

For dark evenings with a blanket and something warm.

  1. Describe your favorite spot in your home for a cool evening.
  2. What is the best soup you have ever had? Who made it?
  3. Write about a night you stayed in and it was exactly what you needed.
  4. What do you like to bake when the weather turns?
  5. Which old movie do you watch every fall?
  6. Write about a cozy evening from your childhood. Who was there, and what was on TV or the radio?
  7. What would a perfect slow Saturday in October include?
  8. Describe the view out your window as the sun goes down earlier.
  9. What could you make with your hands this fall, like a quilt, a scrapbook or a pie?
  10. Write a list of things that make a house feel warm, besides the heat.

Facing your fears journal prompts

October's spooky side is a good excuse to look at real fears, gently. If fear or worry feels heavy for you, our journal prompts for anxiety may help.

Journaling is not a replacement for professional care. If fear or worry is taking over your life, talk to a doctor or counselor. If you are in crisis, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988.

  1. What were you afraid of as a child that does not scare you now? What changed?
  2. Name one fear that holds you back. Where do you think it came from?
  3. What would you do this year if you were not afraid of failing?
  4. Write about the last time fear was actually helpful.
  5. What is the difference between the fears you feel and the risks that are real?
  6. Who do you know who handles fear well? What do they do?
  7. Write a letter to your fear. Tell it what it can and cannot decide for you.
  8. What small step could you take this week toward something that scares you?
  9. When you feel afraid, where do you feel it in your body?
  10. What would you say to a friend who had your exact fear?

More October journal ideas

  • Write one short scary story each week and read them aloud on Halloween.
  • Keep a leaf log with the date the trees near you change color.
  • Draw or describe your Halloween costume plans.
  • Make an autumn page with colors, doodles and favorite lines. See our aesthetic journal ideas for inspiration.
  • Try a few shadow work journal prompts for a deeper look at hidden fears.

Tips for journaling in October

  • Make it a ritual. Light a candle or make a warm drink when you sit down to write.
  • Share the fun. Use the spooky story prompts with friends at a Halloween party.
  • Watch your mood. Shorter days can bring you down. If you feel low, choose the cozy set and write about what helps.
  • Keep going in November. When October ends, continue with November journal prompts.

In the southern hemisphere, October is mid spring. The spring prompts in our April journal prompts may suit your season better.

Questions people ask

What should I journal about in October?

October is peak fall. Write about the changing leaves, cozy evenings, pumpkins and Halloween memories. It is also a good month to look at your fears in a gentle way, since the season is full of spooky fun. End the month with Halloween night: the costumes, the doorbell and the people around you.

What are good Halloween journal prompts?

Good Halloween prompts mix memories and stories. Write about your favorite costume ever, the scariest house on your street, or a haunted house you walked through. Then try a short ghost story set in your own home, or one that begins with a trick or treater knocking at midnight.

What are fall journal prompts for adults?

Fall prompts for adults look at change and comfort. Write about what has changed since last October, what you want to let go of before winter, a fall tradition you have kept for years, and what helps your mood as the days get shorter. Cozy prompts about soup, baking and slow Saturdays are easy wins.

How can I journal about my fears?

Start small. Name one fear, then write where you think it came from and what it has stopped you from doing. Ask what you would say to a friend with the same fear. Then write one small step you could take this week. If fear is overwhelming, talk to a professional. Journaling helps, but it is not a replacement for care.
