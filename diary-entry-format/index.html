The diary entry format, part by part

This is the format most schools teach for diary writing. CBSE and many other school boards use this same basic layout in English writing tasks. Teachers and exam papers can differ on small points, such as where the time goes, so follow your own teacher's version if it is different.

DayWith the dateSaturdaySome formats put it on the same line
TimeWith the date or below9:30 pmEvening is most natural
SalutationNext lineDear Diary,Followed by a comma
BodyMain partTwo or three paragraphsFirst person, past tense

What goes in the body of a diary entry

The body is where most of your marks come from. A strong body has three moves:

  1. Set the scene. Start with one sentence that shows your mood. "Today was the most nerve-wracking day of the term."
  2. Tell the event. Who, what, where and when, in order, in the past tense. Pick the important details.
  3. Reflect. How did it make you feel, and why? What did you learn? What will you do next?

Tenses in a diary entry

UseTenseExample

Feelings right nowSimple presentI feel so proud.
Things that just finishedPresent perfectI have never laughed so much.

Diary format for younger children

Younger students use a simpler version of the same format. A date, Dear Diary, a few sentences about one thing that happened, how it felt, and their name.

Diary format vs letter format

Students often mix up diary writing and letter writing because both are personal. The format is different, and using letter parts in a diary entry can cost marks.

Diary entryInformal letter
Written toYourself or your diaryAnother person
Top of pageDate, day and timeSender's address and date
GreetingDear Diary,Dear Grandma,
ContentYour own day and feelingsNews and questions for the reader
EndingClosing thought and your nameYours lovingly, or similar, and your name
Who reads itOnly youThe person you send it to

Keep the diary entry private in tone. You are not telling someone else your news. You are thinking out loud about your own day. That is why feelings and reflection matter so much in this format.

Diary format for a personal diary

Outside school, the format is much looser. Many people keep only the date and then write freely. Some use bullet points, some write a single line, and some never write Dear Diary at all. The school format is a good habit, though, because the date and a closing thought make old entries easier to understand later. For more on personal diaries, read how to write a diary.

Diary entry format checklist

Before you hand in your diary entry, check each point:

  • Date, day and time are at the top left.
  • Dear Diary is on its own line with a comma.
  • Written in the first person (I, me, my).
  • Events are in the past tense.
  • Feelings are clearly named.
  • There is a closing thought or plan.
  • The signature matches the name in the question.
  • It is close to the word limit.
  • No letter parts like an address or Yours sincerely.

Questions people ask

What is the correct format of a diary entry?

The correct format has the date, day and time at the top left, followed by the salutation Dear Diary. Then comes the body, written in the first person, telling what happened and how you felt. End with a closing thought and your signature. This is the format taught in most schools.

Where do you write the date in a diary entry?

Write the date at the top left of the page, usually with the day and time on the same line or just below. Write the date in full, such as 14 March 2026, rather than using only numbers. Some teachers want the date, day and time on separate lines, so follow your class format.

Is the time necessary in a diary entry?

In many school formats, including the one commonly used for CBSE English, the date, day and time are all expected at the top. In a personal diary the time is optional. If you are writing for an exam, include it to be safe.

What is the diary entry format for class 6?

Class 6 uses the same basic format: date, day and time, Dear Diary, a body of about 100 to 120 words in the first person, a closing line and your name. The topics are usually everyday events like a school trip, a festival or a birthday.

Should a diary entry have a title?

Usually no. A school diary entry starts with the date and Dear Diary, not a title. In a personal diary you can add a title if you like it. Some teachers allow a short heading, so check their instructions. For exams, it is safest to leave it out.
